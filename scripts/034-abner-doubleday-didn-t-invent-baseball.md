# Abner Doubleday Didn't Invent Baseball

## 01 Cold Open

Baseball's official birthplace was picked by a commission that trusted one man's letter, and the man that letter named as the inventor was a student at West Point that year. The story you've probably heard is that Abner Doubleday invented baseball in Cooperstown, New York, in 1839, and it's one of the most famous origin stories in American sports. But people were playing a game called base-ball for nearly a hundred years before that, and the oldest known mention of it in America is a town law telling people not to play it near the windows.

## 02 The Famous Version

So here's where the Doubleday story comes from. In the early 1900s a group called the Mills Commission put out a report on where baseball came from, and that report, dated December 1907 and widely reported as 1908, came down on the side of Abner Doubleday, Cooperstown, 1839. And it stuck, because it's exactly the kind of answer people want, one man, one town, one year.

Now here's the part that matters, the commission's answer rested on a letter. A man named Abner Graves wrote in with the Doubleday story, and the Doubleday claim in that report is built on what Graves said.

## 03 The Trouble With the Letter

And there are two problems with that letter. The first is Graves himself, in 1839, the year he was describing, Abner Graves was five years old. So the whole official story came down to what a man remembered, or said he remembered, from when he was five.

The second problem is Doubleday. Abner Doubleday was at West Point from 1838 to 1842, so in 1839, the year he was supposed to be in Cooperstown inventing baseball, he was a cadet at the military academy, right?

And it isn't just outside critics saying this is a myth. The Library of Congress covers the game's older roots in its baseball exhibition, Britannica calls the Doubleday story discredited, and the Baseball Hall of Fame's own collection calls the Cooperstown story baseball's creation myth. So even the Hall of Fame says it's a myth, right?

## 04 Fair Credit

Now, to be fair to Abner Doubleday, none of this started with him. The claim came from Graves' letter and the commission's report, and Doubleday's name got attached to it. It's not his fault the commission believed it.

And I want to be careful about one more thing, because it's easy to knock down one myth and put up another one. You'll sometimes hear that another man, Alexander Cartwright, is the real father of baseball, and that claim is contested too. So we're not going to swap one inventor for another. The honest answer, and we'll get to it, is that baseball doesn't have a single inventor.

## 05 The Game Before Cooperstown

So let's go back to who was playing it first. The earliest record in this story is a children's book, A Little Pretty Pocket-Book, published in England by John Newbery in 1744. It's got a little verse called Base-Ball, with a woodcut picture next to it, a woodcut being a printed picture carved into a block of wood. And to be clear, that's not modern baseball, and nobody's claiming it is, but it is a game called base-ball, almost a hundred years before Cooperstown.

And there's more. In England, a man named William Bray wrote in his diary that he played base ball on Easter Monday, March 31st, 1755. So it's in a children's book, and then it's in a man's diary, and both of those are more than eighty years before 1839.

Then in America, in 1791, the town of Pittsfield, Massachusetts passed a law, and that law banned people from playing base ball within eighty yards of the new meeting house, to protect its windows. That's the oldest known mention of baseball in America, and it's not a celebration of the game, it's a town trying to stop it from breaking glass, right? And that's forty-eight years before the date the commission picked.

## 06 Nobody Invented It

So if it wasn't Doubleday, the plain answer is it wasn't anybody, at least not one person. Baseball grew out of English bat and ball games, rounders and similar games, and it changed through a lot of hands over a long time before it became the game you'd recognize. Now, the exact family tree, which game turned into which, is something historians have to piece together, so I'm not going to pretend there's a straight line.

But I also don't want to swing too far the other way. You'll hear people say the British invented baseball, and that's not right either. The English games are part of where it came from, and then it kept changing, with no single inventor anywhere in the chain.

## 07 Who Did It First

So here's where it lands. The official story says Abner Doubleday, Cooperstown, 1839, based on one letter from a man who was five at the time, about a man who was at West Point that year. And the record says a game called base-ball was in an English children's book in 1744, in an English diary in 1755, and in a Pittsfield town law in 1791, protecting the windows of the new meeting house.

Baseball is a game a lot of people built, over a long time, and Doubleday wasn't one of the ones the record can show. Somebody did it first, and that's what this channel is about.

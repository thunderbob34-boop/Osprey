## 01 Cold Open

The Eiffel Tower carries Gustave Eiffel's name, and most people assume he designed it, but the idea began with two of his engineers, and the first time the idea was shown to Eiffel, he reportedly said he wasn't interested. Now, that doesn't make him a villain, and we're going to give him a lot of credit before this is over, because his company is the one that won the contest and built the thing. But the sketch that started it was made by an engineer named Maurice Koechlin on June 6th, 1884.

## 02 The Famous Version

The famous version is simple, the tower is named after Eiffel so Eiffel designed it, and that's a fair thing to assume because it's his name on it and it's his company that built it, but the people who started it were two of the chief engineers at that company, Maurice Koechlin and Émile Nouguier.

## 03 The Sketch

Koechlin trained at the Polytechnic in Zurich, where he learned graphic statics, which is a way of working out the forces inside a structure by drawing them, and that's the method the sketch was built on. Koechlin made a rough calculation and a sketch, dated June 6th, 1884, of an iron pylon about 300 meters tall. When the idea was shown to Eiffel, he reportedly said he wasn't interested, and I'm saying reportedly on purpose, because that comes from the official Eiffel Tower site's account. But he did let his engineers keep working on it, and that's what matters here. The ETH Library in Zurich says Koechlin and Nouguier sketched the 300 meter lattice mast in June 1884.

## 04 Sauvestre

Then Eiffel's in-house architect, Stephen Sauvestre, reshaped the design, he added arches and glass pavilions and gave it three levels, and a patent was filed on September 18th, 1884. I'm not going to say whose names were on it, because the sources don't agree.

## 05 The Contest

Then came the competition for the 1889 Exhibition, and there were 107 proposals. The design that won was submitted by Eiffel together with Koechlin, Nouguier and Sauvestre, and Britannica names all four of them as the submitters. So the way to say it fairly is that the idea began with two of his engineers, and the design that won was submitted by the whole team.

## 06 Fair Credit to Eiffel

Now, to be fair to Eiffel, and I really mean this, he deserves a lot of credit. His firm had the bridge-building know-how, he took the financial risk, his team won the contest, and his company built it, right?

And there's more on the know-how side. When Bartholdi needed an internal structure for the Statue of Liberty, he first hired Viollet-le-Duc, and when Viollet-le-Duc died in September 1879, Bartholdi turned to Eiffel. Eiffel designed a central iron pylon, about 92 feet, with a flexible skeleton of girders attached to it. And I want to be clear that the main sources don't say he stole the idea, so we're not going to say that either.

## 07 Who Did It First

So, the plain fact, the idea began with Maurice Koechlin and Émile Nouguier, the two chief engineers at Eiffel's company, and Koechlin made the sketch on June 6th, 1884. Stephen Sauvestre reshaped it, Eiffel's firm took it on and won, and the design was submitted by all four of them. Somebody did it first, and that's what this channel is about.

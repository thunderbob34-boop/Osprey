# The Tailor Who Riveted the First Blue Jeans

## 01 Cold Open

Ask anybody who invented blue jeans and you'll get the same answer, Levi Strauss, because his name is on the pants, but the idea of putting metal rivets in them came from a tailor named Jacob Davis, and Strauss only got involved because Davis wrote him a letter. Now, to be fair, we have to be careful with the word invented, because denim cloth and canvas work pants were already around before any of this, so what Davis came up with was the riveted version, and Strauss was not a thief, he was a supplier and a businessman who said yes. But the patent names Davis as the inventor, and that's the part most people never hear.

## 02 The Famous Version

The famous version is easy to remember, the name on the jeans is Levi Strauss, so people figure Levi Strauss made the first pair, and that's a natural thing to figure. Levi Strauss and Company is named right on the patent, and Davis ended up running production for Strauss, so the company side of the story really is Strauss's, right? What gets lost is where the idea itself came from, and that was a tailor in Reno, Nevada.

## 03 Levi Strauss

Let's start with Strauss, because he deserves a fair hearing. The company's own history says he was born in Bavaria in 1829, and that he arrived in San Francisco in 1853 and opened a wholesale dry goods business, selling clothing, blankets and other goods to small general stores across the American West. So by the time Jacob Davis comes into this, Strauss isn't a man looking for something to do, he's already a supplier with customers all over the West, and that matters for what happens next.

## 04 Jacob Davis

Jacob Davis was a Latvian-born tailor, and the company's history and the Online Nevada encyclopedia have him settling in Reno, Nevada in 1868, where he made things like tents and wagon covers. As the company and museum account of his story tells it, in December 1870 he reinforced a pair of canvas work pants with copper rivets for a customer whose pockets kept tearing, and the company's version and the Nevada encyclopedia both say the one who asked for sturdy pants was the wife of a laborer. Now, that December 1870 date comes from the company and museum account, the patent itself only dates the grant in 1873, so that's worth keeping in mind. What the patent does describe is a rivet at the ends of the seams, to take the strain off the thread, and that's the whole idea, the spot that tears gets a piece of metal holding it together.

## 05 The Letter

Here's where Strauss comes in, and it's a detail I like, because Strauss's company was the one that supplied Davis with his cloth. Davis had an idea worth protecting, but he didn't have the money for the patent, so in 1872 he wrote to his cloth supplier, Levi Strauss, and offered him a partnership. He was offering Strauss a share in exchange for the money to patent the idea and then to make the product, and that's how Smithsonian Magazine tells it.

The patent is called "Improvement in Fastening Pocket-Openings," it's US 139,121, and it was granted on May 20th, 1873, with Jacob Davis of Reno, Nevada as the inventor and half of it assigned to Levi Strauss and Company. So it's a shared one, and the inventor on the patent is Davis.

## 06 Running Production

After the patent, Davis moved to San Francisco and ran production for Strauss, so the tailor ended up running production for the businessman he'd written to. The patent gave the two of them the exclusive right to make riveted trousers for 17 years, and what they sold was called waist overalls, which was the old name for them, and it wasn't until 1890 that the company renamed that pair Lot 501. So the number people know on a pair of jeans came later, after the partnership had already been going for years.

## 07 Giving Strauss His Due

Now here's the fair part, because I don't want this to sound like Strauss took something that wasn't his. He was the financier, the supplier and the business partner, and Davis went to him on purpose, with an offer, because Davis lacked the money to file the patent himself. And the company that Strauss ran is the one named on the patent next to Davis, with half of it assigned to that company. So this isn't a story about a theft, it's a story about a tailor with an idea and a supplier with the means to back it.

## 08 Who Did It First

So when you put it all together, denim and work pants were already around, and the riveted version came from Jacob Davis, a Latvian-born tailor in Reno who reinforced a pair of canvas work pants with copper rivets, as the company and museum account tells it, in December 1870. He wrote to his cloth supplier Levi Strauss in 1872, the patent was granted in 1873 with Davis as the inventor and half assigned to Strauss's company, and Strauss was a partner, not a thief. Somebody did it first, and that's what this channel is about.

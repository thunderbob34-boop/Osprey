# Kodak's Film Had a Rival Inventor: Hannibal Goodwin

- Say Goodwin as GOOD-win, Reichenbach as RYE-ken-bock, Newark as NEW-ark, pellicle as PEL-ih-kul.

## 01 Cold Open

George Eastman and Kodak put celluloid roll film on the market in 1889, and Eastman became the face of flexible photographic film, but a clergyman in Newark, New Jersey, named Hannibal Goodwin had filed for a patent on a celluloid photographic film on May 2nd, 1887, two years before Kodak sold any, and in 1914 a federal appeals court held that Kodak had infringed his patent. I want to be careful about how big a claim that is, because Eastman got his film to the market first, with his own patents, and he's the one who turned it into a business, so this isn't a story about a fraud, and we'll give him full credit for what he did.

## 02 The Famous Version

The famous version is Eastman and Kodak, who put celluloid roll film on the market in 1889 and became the face of flexible photographic film. Eastman reached the market with his own patents, and that included a process from 1889 by his chemist, Henry Reichenbach. Now here's the part that matters, nothing in this story says Eastman copied anybody, and we're not going to say it. What it says is that somebody else had filed first.

## 03 Hannibal Goodwin

Hannibal Goodwin was an Episcopal clergyman in Newark, New Jersey, and on May 2nd, 1887, he filed an application for a celluloid photographic film. His patent was US Patent 610,861, it's titled "Photographic pellicle and process of producing same," and it wasn't granted until September 13th, 1898. So he filed in 1887, Kodak was selling roll film in 1889, and the patent didn't come through until 1898.

## 04 The Court

That question went to court, and the case was called Goodwin Film and Camera Company against Eastman Kodak Company. A 1913 decree held that certain claims of the Goodwin patent were valid and infringed, and in 1914 the Second Circuit court of appeals affirmed that decree. Infringed just means Kodak had used something those claims covered, and the court held it had, right? So that's the sentence we can say flat out, in 1914 a federal appeals court held that Kodak had infringed Goodwin's patent.

Now, to be fair, I want to keep that claim the size it really is. The court held certain claims of the patent valid and infringed, and the patent is about flexible celluloid photographic film, so we're not saying Goodwin invented film.

## 05 The Plain Version

One more thing, you'll read different accounts of the money, and the accounts disagree about how much was paid and who ended up with it, so we're not going to put a number on it, and we won't guess. Here's what doesn't change, Eastman reached the market first with his own patents and he's the one who made it a business, and Goodwin, a clergyman in Newark, filed first, in 1887, and a federal appeals court held in 1914 that Kodak had infringed his patent.

## 01 Cold Open

George Eastman and Kodak put celluloid roll film on the market in 1889, and Eastman became the face of flexible photographic film, but a clergyman in Newark, New Jersey, named Hannibal Goodwin had filed for a patent on a celluloid photographic film on May 2nd, 1887, two years before Kodak sold any celluloid film, and in 1914 a federal appeals court held that Kodak had infringed his patent. I want to be careful about how big a claim that is, because Eastman got his film to the market first, with his own patents, and he's the one who turned it into a business, so this isn't a story about a fraud, and we'll give him full credit for what he did.

## 02 The Famous Version

The famous version is Eastman and Kodak, and here's some background on it. The first Kodak camera came out in 1888, and the Smithsonian's collection describes it as selling for twenty-five dollars with a roll of film already loaded, good for a hundred exposures. When the roll was done you mailed the whole camera back to the factory, and for ten dollars they developed and printed the pictures and reloaded it, and that first film was paper-based. The transparent celluloid film came in 1889, and that's the version the Eastman company put on the market and became the face of.

Eastman reached the market with his own patents, and that included a process from 1889 by his chemist, Henry Reichenbach. Now here's the part that matters, nothing in this story says Eastman copied anybody, and we're not going to say it, what it says is that Goodwin had filed for his patent in 1887, before any of that celluloid film was on sale.

## 03 Hannibal Goodwin

Hannibal Goodwin was an Episcopal clergyman in Newark, New Jersey, and the New Jersey historical markers list says he worked at the House of Prayer rectory, an Episcopal church in Newark. On May 2nd, 1887 he filed an application for a celluloid photographic film. His patent was US Patent 610,861, it's titled "Photographic pellicle and process of producing same," and it wasn't granted until September 13th, 1898, so he filed in 1887, Kodak was selling celluloid roll film in 1889, and the patent didn't come through until 1898, which is eleven years after he filed.

## 04 The Court

The patent was fought over in court, in a case called Goodwin Film and Camera Company against Eastman Kodak Company. A decree in 1913 held certain claims of the Goodwin patent valid and infringed, those were claims 1, 6, 8, 10 and 12, and in 1914 the Second Circuit court of appeals affirmed that decree. Infringed just means Kodak had used something those claims covered, and the court held it had, right? So that's the sentence we can say flat out, in 1914 a federal appeals court held that Kodak had infringed Goodwin's patent.

# Kodak's Film Had a Rival Inventor: Hannibal Goodwin

READING NOTES
- Runtime is about 5 minutes at a relaxed pace, roughly 710 spoken words.
- Tone is calm and matter-of-fact. Eastman is not a villain in this story. He reached the market first and he built the business, and the script says so.
- Say Goodwin as GOOD-win, Reichenbach as RYE-ken-bock, Newark as NEW-ark, pellicle as PEL-ih-kul, Ansco as ANN-sko.
- Every hedge in here is on purpose. Read "flexible celluloid photographic film", "certain claims", "held that Kodak had infringed", "paper-based", "as the opinion describes it" and "we won't guess" exactly as written, because they are what keep the channel credible.
- Never say Goodwin invented film, never give a dollar amount or say who was paid, and never say Eastman copied him.
- Visuals: nothing sensitive. Patent drawings, period photos, and text cards are fine.
- Good on-screen text moments are May 2 1887, 1888, 1889, 1898, 1910, 1913, 1914, Patent 610,861, and claims 1, 6, 8, 10 and 12.

## 01 Cold Open

George Eastman and Kodak put celluloid roll film on the market in 1889, and Eastman became the face of flexible photographic film, but a clergyman in Newark, New Jersey, named Hannibal Goodwin had filed for a patent on a celluloid photographic film on May 2nd, 1887, two years before Kodak sold any celluloid film, and in 1914 a federal appeals court held that Kodak had infringed his patent. I want to be careful about how big a claim that is, because Eastman got his film to the market first, with his own patents, and he's the one who turned it into a business, so this isn't a story about a fraud, and we'll give him full credit for what he did.

## 02 The Famous Version

The famous version is Eastman and Kodak, and there's a step in it that people skip. The first Kodak camera came out in 1888, and the Smithsonian's collection describes it as selling for twenty-five dollars with a roll of film already loaded, good for a hundred exposures. When the roll was done you mailed the whole camera back to the factory, and for ten dollars they developed and printed the pictures and sent the camera back with fresh film in it. That first film was paper-based, and the transparent celluloid film came in 1889, which is the version the Eastman company put on the market and became the face of.

Eastman reached the market with his own patents, and that included a process from 1889 by his chemist, Henry Reichenbach. Now here's the part that matters, nothing in this story says Eastman copied anybody, and we're not going to say it. What it says is that somebody else had filed first.

## 03 Hannibal Goodwin

Hannibal Goodwin was an Episcopal clergyman in Newark, New Jersey, and the New Jersey historical marker list ties his film work to the rectory of the House of Prayer, an Episcopal church in Newark. On May 2nd, 1887, he filed an application for a celluloid photographic film. His patent was US Patent 610,861, it's titled "Photographic pellicle and process of producing same," and it wasn't granted until September 13th, 1898. So he filed in 1887, Kodak was selling celluloid roll film in 1889, and the patent didn't come through until 1898, which is eleven years after he filed.

## 04 The Court

That question went to court, and the case was called Goodwin Film and Camera Company against Eastman Kodak Company. The patent had been assigned to the Goodwin Film and Camera Company in 1898, and in 1910 a company called Ansco bought all of that company's outstanding stock, so by the time of the case the name on the suit was a company, not the clergyman himself. A decree dated September 9th, 1913 held certain claims of the Goodwin patent valid and infringed, and those were claims 1, 6, 8, 10 and 12, and in 1914 the Second Circuit court of appeals affirmed that decree.

Infringed just means Kodak had used something those claims covered, and the court held it had, right? So that's the sentence we can say flat out, in 1914 a federal appeals court held that Kodak had infringed Goodwin's patent.

## 05 What the Court Said About 1887

There's one more line from the appeals court that's worth passing on. As the opinion describes it, the application was filed in 1887, and given the state of the art at that time, the invention was one of more than ordinary merit, and the patent was entitled to a liberal construction. I'm giving you that as the court's own wording.

Now, to be fair, I want to keep the claim the size it really is. The court held certain claims of the patent valid and infringed, and the patent is about flexible celluloid photographic film, so we're not saying Goodwin invented film.

## 06 The Plain Version

One more thing, you'll read different accounts of the money, and the accounts disagree about how much was paid and who ended up with it, so we're not going to put a number on it, and we won't guess. Here's what doesn't change, Eastman reached the market first with his own patents and he's the one who made it a business, and Goodwin, a clergyman in Newark, filed first, in 1887, and a federal appeals court held in 1914 that Kodak had infringed his patent.

# Halley Didn't Discover Halley's Comet

READING NOTES
- Runtime is about 4 to 5 minutes at a relaxed pace, roughly 711 spoken words. The research is short, so this one runs short on purpose.
- Tone is calm and a little warm. Halley is not the villain here, he never claimed to discover it, and the script says so.
- Say Shiji as SHUR-jee, Bayeux as by-YUH (or bay-YOO), Edmond as ED-mund.
- Every hedge in here is on purpose. Read "first certain", "nearly two thousand years", "technically not a tapestry, it's an embroidery", "earlier sightings are uncertain" and "never claimed" exactly as written. Don't say anyone saw it before 240 BCE.
- Visuals: the comet panel of the Bayeux Tapestry, period portraits of Halley, NASA and ESA comet images, and text cards. Don't name who spotted the 1758 return on screen, that wasn't checked.
- Good on-screen text moments are 240 BCE, broom star, 1066, 1531 / 1607 / 1682, 1705, 1742, and 1758.

## 01 Cold Open

Halley's Comet was being written down by Chinese court astronomers nearly two thousand years before Edmond Halley was born. Everybody knows the name, and it has his name on it, but he didn't discover it, and to be fair to him, he never said he did. What Halley actually did was work out that this comet keeps coming back, and that's a real achievement, but the first people to write it down were in China, and they called it a broom star.

## 02 The Famous Version

So here's how most people picture it. There's a comet called Halley's Comet, so somebody named Halley must have found it, right? That's how names on things usually work, you find it, you get your name on it. And Edmond Halley was a real astronomer and the comet really is named after him, but the name isn't there because he was the first to see it. People had been seeing this comet and writing it down for a very long time before Halley was ever born.

## 03 The Broom Star

The first certain record of Halley's Comet is from China, in 240 BCE, in a book called the Shiji, which in English is usually called the Records of the Grand Historian, and the Chinese astronomers called it a broom star. NASA points to that record, and so does the European Space Agency.

Now, you'll sometimes hear about sightings even earlier than that, and the honest thing to say is that those earlier sightings are uncertain. So 240 BCE is the first certain one, that's the one we can be sure was this comet, and it's nearly two thousand years before Halley's time.

## 04 1066

And it kept coming back, and people kept writing it down. In 1066 it showed up again, before the Norman Conquest of England, and it ended up stitched into the Bayeux Tapestry. So if you've ever seen the Bayeux Tapestry, there's a comet in it, right, and that comet is the one that would later get Halley's name.

Oh, one small thing, the Bayeux Tapestry is technically not a tapestry, it's an embroidery. A tapestry is woven, and an embroidery is stitched onto cloth, and this one is stitched. But everybody calls it the Bayeux Tapestry, so that's what we'll call it.

## 05 What Halley Actually Did

Now here's the part that matters, because his name is on it for a reason. In 1705, in a book called A Synopsis of the Astronomy of Comets, Halley argued that the comets seen in 1531, 1607 and 1682 weren't three different comets, they were one comet, the same one, coming back. And if you look at those dates, the gaps between them are about seventy-five or seventy-six years.

And the reason a comet can come back at all is that it's going around the sun, the same as a planet, just on a long stretched-out loop, so it swings in close where we can see it, then heads way back out for decades, and then it comes around again. So what Halley was saying is, these aren't three comets that showed up and left, this is one comet on a loop, and if it's on a loop, you can say when it's coming back.

And then he predicted it would come back again, in 1758, and that's a real prediction, a date in the future that could turn out to be right or wrong. And proving that a comet comes back, that's genuine, that's his, and that's why the name is fair.

## 06 He Never Saw It

And here's the sad part, Halley never saw his comet come back, because he died in 1742, sixteen years before it returned, right on schedule, in 1758. So the prediction came true, and the man who made it wasn't there to see it, he'd been gone for sixteen years.

## 07 The Broom Star Came First

So the plain version is this. Chinese astronomers recorded this comet in 240 BCE in the Shiji and called it a broom star, it was stitched into the Bayeux Tapestry in 1066, and Edmond Halley, in 1705, showed that it was one comet coming back, and he predicted its return in 1758 and didn't live to see it, and he never claimed to have discovered it. Somebody did it first, and that somebody was writing it down in China nearly two thousand years before Halley was born.

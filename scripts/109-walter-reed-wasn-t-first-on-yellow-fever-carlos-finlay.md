# Walter Reed Wasn't First on Yellow Fever: Carlos Finlay

## 01 Cold Open

Walter Reed is widely credited with discovering that mosquitoes spread yellow fever, and his Army board proved it in Havana in 1900, but nineteen years earlier, in August 1881, a Cuban doctor named Carlos Finlay stood up in front of Havana's medical academy and said that mosquitoes spread yellow fever. Now, I want to be exact about the wording, because Finlay proposed it and Reed proved it, and those are two different things. Both of them matter, and this is a story about how the two fit together.

## 02 The Famous Version

The famous version is Walter Reed and the U.S. Army Yellow Fever Board in Havana in 1900, and they get the credit because their experiments on volunteers confirmed that mosquitoes transmit the disease. That's a real achievement. But the idea they were testing was not theirs, and that's the part that gets left out.

## 03 Carlos Finlay

Carlos Finlay was a Cuban physician, and on August 14th, 1881 he read a paper to the Royal Academy of Medical, Physical, and Natural Sciences of Havana. In it he proposed that a mosquito, which we now identify as Aedes aegypti, carries yellow fever from one person to another. As far as the modern scientific record goes, Finlay was the first to propose the mosquito as the carrier of yellow fever, and I'm saying it that carefully on purpose.

And the reception was skeptical, so the idea sat there for years before anyone proved it.

## 04 Reed Tests It

Now, to be fair to Reed, he wasn't just repeating Finlay. Reed was skeptical at first, but he tested the theory, and his board in 1900 refined the method with controlled experiments on volunteers, and in the end it confirmed mosquito transmission. Reed also acknowledged Finlay's contribution, so this isn't a story about theft.

And there's something else that's fair to say about Finlay's side. His own experiments were inconsistent and had methodological flaws, and notably, nobody yet knew about the extrinsic incubation period, which just means a mosquito needs some time after feeding on a sick person before it can pass the disease on. So the proof really was Reed's.

## 05 The Nobel Prize

It took until 1900, nineteen years after Finlay's paper, for Reed's team to prove he was right. Finlay was nominated for the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine several times, and he never won, right? That's the plain fact of it.

## 06 Who Did It First

So, the plain fact, on August 14th, 1881 Carlos Finlay proposed to Havana's medical academy that a mosquito carries yellow fever, and in 1900 Walter Reed's board proved it with controlled experiments on volunteers. Finlay proposed it, Reed proved it, and Reed acknowledged Finlay along the way. Somebody did it first, and that's what this channel is about.

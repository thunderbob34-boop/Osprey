## 01 Cold Open

Walter Reed is widely credited with discovering that mosquitoes spread yellow fever, and his Army board proved it in Havana in 1900, but nineteen years earlier, in August 1881, a Cuban doctor named Carlos Finlay stood up in front of Havana's medical academy and said that mosquitoes spread yellow fever. Now, I want to be exact about the wording, because Finlay proposed it and Reed proved it, and those are two different things. Both of them matter, and this is a story about how the two fit together.

## 02 The Famous Version

The famous version is Walter Reed and the U.S. Army Yellow Fever Board in Havana in 1900, and they get the credit because their experiments on volunteers confirmed that mosquitoes transmit the disease. The Army put that board together in May 1900, and it had four members, Reed, who led it, and James Carroll, Aristides Agramonte, and Jesse Lazear. That's a real achievement. But the idea they were testing was not theirs, and that's the part that gets left out.

## 03 Carlos Finlay

Carlos Finlay was a Cuban physician, born in Camagüey, and he studied medicine in Philadelphia at Jefferson Medical College, where he graduated in 1855. He started his own practice in Havana in 1864, and yellow fever epidemics kept coming through Havana, so this was not an abstract question for him.

Then on August 14th, 1881 he read a paper to the Royal Academy of Medical, Physical, and Natural Sciences of Havana. In it he proposed that a mosquito, which we now identify as Aedes aegypti, carries yellow fever from one person to another. As far as the modern scientific record goes, Finlay was the first to propose the mosquito as the carrier of yellow fever, and I'm saying it that carefully on purpose.

And the reception was skeptical, so the idea sat there for years before anyone proved it.

## 04 Reed Tests It

Now, to be fair to Reed, he wasn't just repeating Finlay. When the board got to Cuba in 1900, Finlay tried to persuade Reed that mosquitoes were the carrier, and Reed was skeptical at first, but he decided to investigate it. His board refined the method with controlled experiments on volunteers, and in the end it confirmed mosquito transmission. Reed also acknowledged Finlay's contribution, so this isn't a story about theft.

## 05 Why the Proof Was Reed's

And there's something else that's fair to say about Finlay's side. His own experiments were inconsistent and had methodological flaws, and notably, nobody yet knew about the extrinsic incubation period, which just means a mosquito needs some time after feeding on a sick person before it can pass the disease on.

Now here's the part that matters, because that gap had been spotted by then, by a doctor named Henry Rose Carter. In 1898 Carter showed that after a first case of yellow fever there was a wait of about ten to sixteen days before secondary cases appeared, and he called that the period of extrinsic incubation. Reed said later that Carter's work in Mississippi did more than anything else to impress on him that an intermediate host mattered in yellow fever. And the later analyses of Finlay's experiments say he didn't consistently feed mosquitoes on patients in the first three days of their illness, and he fed them on his volunteers too soon afterward, and he never considered that the mosquito itself needed time. The board's experiments put that gap at twelve days. So the proof really was Reed's.

## 06 What the Proof Cost

The proof wasn't free, either. On August 27th, 1900, an infected mosquito was allowed to feed on James Carroll, and he came down with a severe case of yellow fever. Then Jesse Lazear was bitten, he developed yellow fever, and he died on September 25th, 1900. After that the rest of the board built an isolated camp outside Havana and named it Camp Lazear, and the controlled experiments on volunteers took place there, starting in November 1900.

One of those volunteers was a private named John Kissinger, who reportedly developed the first controlled case, and he and another volunteer, John Moran, signed up without asking for any payment.

## 07 The Nobel Prize

It took until 1900, nineteen years after Finlay's paper, for Reed's team to prove he was right. In 1902 Finlay became the first Director of Health of the Republic of Cuba, he held that job until 1909, and he died in Havana on August 20th, 1915. He was nominated for the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine several times, and he never won, right? That's the plain fact of it.

## 08 Who Did It First

So, the plain fact, on August 14th, 1881 Carlos Finlay proposed to Havana's medical academy that a mosquito carries yellow fever, and in 1900 Walter Reed's board proved it with controlled experiments on volunteers. Finlay proposed it, Reed proved it, and Reed acknowledged Finlay along the way. Somebody did it first, and that's what this channel is about.

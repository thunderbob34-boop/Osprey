## 01 Cold Open

Kellogg is the name most people think of when they think of breakfast cereal, but the man who made the first cold cereal sued Kellogg over its name. And that first cold cereal was so hard you had to soak it in milk overnight before you could eat it. John Harvey Kellogg did something real in the history of breakfast, and we'll give him credit for it, but he wasn't first.

## 02 The Famous Version

The version a lot of people carry around is pretty simple, Kellogg invented breakfast cereal. And you can see how that happens, the name is so tied to cereal, and when one name gets that big it's easy to assume it was there from the start, right?

Now, Kellogg's real fame came from corn flakes, in the 1890s. That's the thing his name is actually tied to. But the idea that Kellogg invented cold cereal in the first place, that's the part that isn't true, because by the 1890s there had already been cold cereal for about thirty years.

## 03 Dr. Jackson's Granula

The man who made it was James Caleb Jackson. He ran a water-cure sanitarium, which was a kind of health resort, in Dansville, New York. And in 1863 he came up with something he called Granula.

Here's how he made it. He took graham flour, which is a coarse whole wheat flour, and baked it into brittle cakes. Then he broke those up into crumbs, and baked them again. And what you ended up with was so hard that you had to soak it in milk overnight before you could eat it. So this was not a pour-it-and-eat-it situation, this was plan-ahead-the-night-before cereal.

And Britannica says Jackson's Granula was probably the first ready-to-eat cereal. I want to keep that word, probably, because that's how Britannica puts it. But it's the earliest one in this story by a long way, and it's 1863.

## 04 The Name Fight

Now here's the part that matters. In the late 1870s or early 1880s, John Harvey Kellogg came out with a cereal of his own that was a lot like Jackson's. And he called it granula, the same name.

So Jackson sued, and Kellogg changed the name. He changed it to granola, with an o. So the word granola, that spelling, goes back to Kellogg having to stop using Jackson's name.

And I want to be careful here, because the lawsuit is one of those things that's easy to dress up. Britannica and the Smithsonian both say Jackson sued and Kellogg renamed it, and that's what we know. The sources don't give the court details, so I'm not going to tell you what the ruling was or how much money was involved, because nobody's shown me, and I'm not going to guess.

## 05 Fair Credit to Kellogg

Now, to be fair to Kellogg, he's a big part of the history of breakfast cereal, he just isn't the start of it. His real fame comes from corn flakes, in the 1890s, and that's a real thing he's remembered for.

So the fair way to say it is that Kellogg is the corn flakes name, and that's earned. What he didn't do is invent cold cereal, because James Caleb Jackson had his Granula in Dansville in 1863. And even the granola part, the name that stuck, came from Kellogg having to back off Jackson's name.

## 06 Who Did It First

So here's how it lines up. In 1863, James Caleb Jackson made Granula at his sanitarium in Dansville, New York, out of graham flour baked, crumbled and baked again, and it was so hard you had to soak it overnight. Britannica calls it probably the first ready-to-eat cereal. In the late 1870s or early 1880s, Kellogg made a similar cereal with the same name, Jackson sued, and Kellogg changed it to granola. And Kellogg's real fame came later, with corn flakes, in the 1890s.

Somebody did it first, and that's what this channel is about.

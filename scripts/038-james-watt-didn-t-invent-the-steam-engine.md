# James Watt Didn't Invent the Steam Engine

## 01 Cold Open

James Watt didn't invent the steam engine, and by the time he patented his famous fix, the kind of engine he was fixing had been working for fifty-seven years. Watt's name is on the unit of power, and he's widely called the inventor of the steam engine, and what he did really was a breakthrough, and we'll give him full credit for it. But the engine he improved was already working in England before he was even born, and it was built by a man almost nobody has heard of.

## 02 The Famous Version

The version most of us picked up goes something like this, James Watt invents the steam engine, and that kicks off the age of steam. And you can see why his name stuck, it's literally a unit of measurement, every time you see a watt on a lightbulb box or a microwave, that's him. When your name turns into a unit, people tend to assume you started the whole thing, right?

But Britannica and the American Society of Mechanical Engineers, ASME, both say the same thing, Watt improved the steam engine rather than inventing it. And the engine he improved was built by Thomas Newcomen.

## 03 Newcomen's Engine

Thomas Newcomen's engine was working near Dudley Castle, in Tipton, in the West Midlands of England, in 1712. ASME calls it the first successful Newcomen engine.

Now, his engine was what's called an atmospheric engine, and the name tells you how it works. You fill a cylinder with steam, then you cool the steam so it turns back into water, and that leaves a partial vacuum inside the cylinder. And then the weight of the air outside, the atmosphere, pushes the piston down. So the steam isn't really doing the pushing, it's the air, and the steam is just how you make the vacuum.

And here's the catch with that. To cool the steam, you're cooling the cylinder, and then to fill it with steam again for the next stroke, you have to heat the whole cylinder back up. So every single stroke, you're heating it, cooling it, heating it again, and that burns a lot of fuel, right?

## 04 Watt Arrives

Now, keep the dates in mind here. Newcomen's engine was working in 1712, and James Watt wasn't born until 1736, so that engine had been around for twenty-four years before Watt was even born, right?

Then in 1764 Watt saw that waste problem in the Newcomen engine, and in 1765 he came up with the fix, what's called the separate condenser. The idea is right there in the name, instead of cooling the steam in the main cylinder, you send it off to a separate chamber and cool it there. So the main cylinder can stay hot, and you're not heating it back up every stroke.

And he patented it in 1769, that's fifty-seven years after Newcomen's engine was working near Dudley Castle, so by the time Watt's famous improvement was on paper with a patent, the kind of engine he was improving had been working for over half a century.

## 05 Fair Credit to Watt

Now, to be fair to Watt, the separate condenser was a real breakthrough, and I don't want that to get lost. Britannica says it cut fuel use by about seventy-five percent, and that's not a small tweak. An engine that burns about a quarter of the fuel is a very different thing to run than one that doesn't, so a lot of what made steam power practical runs straight through Watt's fix.

So the fair way to say it is that Watt took an engine that already worked and made it far more efficient. He was the improver who made it practical, but what he wasn't was the man who invented it, because Newcomen had one working before Watt was born.

## 06 Who Did It First

So here's how it lines up. Thomas Newcomen had his atmospheric engine working near Dudley Castle in 1712. James Watt was born in 1736, saw the waste problem in Newcomen's engine in 1764, came up with the separate condenser in 1765, and patented it in 1769, fifty-seven years after Newcomen's engine was already at work.

Watt's name is on the unit of power, and he earned a big part of that, but the steam engine he improved was Newcomen's, so somebody did it first, and Newcomen had his engine working before Watt was even born.

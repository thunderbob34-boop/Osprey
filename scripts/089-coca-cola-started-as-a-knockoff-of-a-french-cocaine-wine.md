## 01 Cold Open

Coca-Cola began as an American copy of a French wine laced with coca, one that even a pope endorsed. Most people think of John Pemberton in Atlanta in 1886 as the man who invented a brand new kind of drink, and he did invent the Coca-Cola formula, we'll give him that. But before Coca-Cola, the world's favorite coca drink was a French wine called Vin Mariani, and Pemberton's first version was a copy of it.

## 02 The Famous Version

So here's the version most people know. In 1886, in Atlanta, a man named John Pemberton came up with a new tonic drink, and that drink became Coca-Cola, and it went on to be one of the most famous brands in the world. And a tonic, back then, was a drink sold as something that was good for you, sort of halfway between a drink and a medicine.

And it's easy to see why that's the story people remember, right? Coca-Cola is huge, and when something is that big, people want it to have a simple beginning, one man, one city, one new idea, but the idea wasn't new, and it wasn't even American.

## 03 Vin Mariani

The story really starts in 1863 with a chemist named Angelo Mariani, who was born on Corsica. That year Mariani launched a drink called Vin Mariani, and it was Bordeaux wine with coca extract in it. Coca is the plant that cocaine comes from, so this was wine with coca in it, sold as a tonic, and it sold all over the world.

Now, to be careful about it, sources disagree on whether Vin Mariani was the very first coca wine, and the way the medical history sources put it, it was not the first cocaine tonic, but the most successful. So the fair way to say it is that Vin Mariani was the first big hit.

And it was a big hit, a really, really big one. Pope Leo the Thirteenth liked it enough to award it a Vatican gold medal, and he let his face appear in its ads. So you've got a French coca wine with a pope in the advertising, right? And this isn't some internet legend, PBS NewsHour has covered the pope and the wine, a peer-reviewed paper on the early history of cocaine writes about the gold medal, and the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History has a Vin Mariani object in its collection. So the wine was real, the medal was real, and the pope in the ads was real.

## 04 French Wine Coca

Now here's the part that matters. Before there was Coca-Cola, John Pemberton made a drink called French Wine Coca, sometimes also called Peruvian Wine Coca, and it imitated Vin Mariani. It was a coca wine, the same idea, and Pemberton added kola nut to it. The National Library of Medicine, in its exhibition on the history of cocaine, describes Pemberton's French Wine Coca as an inferior imitation of Mariani's wine, and that's their word, imitation, not ours.

So the question is how closely Pemberton copied Mariani's actual recipe, as opposed to the general idea, and nobody can really say for sure. But the idea, the coca wine tonic, that came from Mariani, and Pemberton's version came after it and copied it.

## 05 Prohibition

And there's more, because then Atlanta changed the rules. In 1886 Atlanta adopted prohibition, which means alcohol was banned, and Pemberton's French Wine Coca was a wine, so that was a problem. So Pemberton reworked it into something without alcohol, a non-alcoholic version, and that non-alcoholic drink was Coca-Cola.

So Coca-Cola, one of the most famous soft drinks there is, was what Pemberton made when Atlanta banned alcohol, and the wine it replaced was itself an imitation of a French one. And later on, Coca-Cola went on to overtake Vin Mariani.

## 06 To Be Fair

Now, to be fair to Pemberton, he did invent the Coca-Cola formula itself, that's his, and nobody's saying he didn't. What came first was the coca tonic idea and the product he copied. So the honest way to say it is that Coke was built on Mariani's idea, not that Pemberton stole anything, the National Library of Medicine says imitated, and we'll stick with imitated and copied.

And Mariani deserves his due too, because he's the one who made a coca tonic into a worldwide hit, gold medal from a pope and all, and he launched it in 1863, years before Coca-Cola.

## 07 Who Did It First

So let's put it in order. In 1863 Angelo Mariani launched Vin Mariani, Bordeaux wine with coca extract, and it sold worldwide, and Pope Leo the Thirteenth gave it a Vatican gold medal and let his face appear in its ads. Then John Pemberton made French Wine Coca, an imitation of Mariani's wine with kola nut added. In 1886 Atlanta adopted prohibition, and Pemberton reworked his wine into the non-alcoholic Coca-Cola.

So Pemberton invented the formula, and Coca-Cola is his, but the drink it grew out of was a copy of a French coca wine. Somebody did it first, and his name was Angelo Mariani.

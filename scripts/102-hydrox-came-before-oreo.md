# Hydrox Came Before Oreo

READING NOTES
- Runtime is about 3 minutes at a relaxed pace, roughly 408 spoken words.
- Tone is light and matter-of-fact. This one is told with dates only, so let the dates carry it and do not add any attitude about Oreo.
- Say Hydrox as HY-drox, Nabisco as NAB-iss-co, KCUR as K-C-U-R.
- Every hedge in here is on purpose. Read "widely treated as", "came four years later", "as far as these sources go" and "was actually introduced first, in 1908" exactly as written. Never call the Oreo a copy, a knockoff or an imitation, because that is an inference the research does not support.

## 01 Cold Open

Oreo is widely treated as the original chocolate cream sandwich cookie, but it wasn't the first cookie of its kind. A Kansas City company had one on shelves in 1908, and the Oreo came four years later, in 1912. Now, I'm not going to call the Oreo a copy, and we'll get to why, but the dates are the dates, and the cookie that came first was called Hydrox.

## 02 The Famous Version

The Oreo is Nabisco's cookie, and according to Knowledge at Wharton, the business school publication from the University of Pennsylvania, the first Oreo sale was on March 6th, 1912. So if you treat the Oreo as the original, which a lot of people do, then 1912 is where the story starts, and that's the version most of us have heard.

## 03 Hydrox

Hydrox was introduced in 1908 by Sunshine Biscuits of Kansas City, and that same Wharton article says Hydrox was actually introduced first, in 1908. NPR has it the same way, Hydrox in 1908 and Oreo in 1912, and KCUR, the Kansas City public radio station, also puts the debut in 1908 and says it came from a Kansas City company. The Smithsonian's National Museum of American History holds records of the Sunshine Biscuit Company in its archives. And as far as these sources go, Hydrox was the first chocolate sandwich cookie of its kind in the US, though I can't give you the month it came out, because the sources I found give the year and not the month.

## 04 What We Can Say

Now, here's the part where I have to be careful. Some web pages call the Oreo a knockoff, and I'm not going to say that, because it's somebody's inference and it isn't a documented fact. What the sources actually give us is the dates, Hydrox in 1908 and the Oreo in 1912, so the fair way to say it is that the Oreo came four years later, and that's all. It doesn't tell us whether the Oreo was a response to Hydrox, right? And I'd rather say less than say something I can't back up.

## 05 Close

So here's the plain version. Hydrox, from Sunshine Biscuits of Kansas City, came out in 1908, and the Oreo came four years later, in 1912. As far as these sources go, Hydrox was the first chocolate sandwich cookie of its kind in the US, and the Oreo is the follow-up and not the original. Somebody did it first, and it was a Kansas City company.

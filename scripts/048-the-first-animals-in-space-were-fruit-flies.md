## 01 Cold Open

The first animals we know reached space weren't dogs or monkeys, they were fruit flies, riding a captured German rocket. Most people would tell you the first animal in space was Laika, the dog, and Laika really was a first, just not that first. More than ten years before Laika, a canister of fruit flies went up about 67 miles over New Mexico, came back down on a parachute, and the flies came home alive.

## 02 Laika

So let's start with the famous one. Laika went up on Sputnik 2 on November 3rd, 1957, and Laika was the first animal to orbit the Earth. That's a real first, and it's the one that stuck, and orbit is a big deal, going all the way around the planet instead of just up and back down.

But first to orbit and first to reach space are two different things, right? And the first animals we know of to reach space had already been up and back a decade earlier.

## 03 The Fruit Flies

On February 20th, 1947, the United States launched a captured German V-2 rocket from White Sands, New Mexico, and on board were fruit flies and seeds. The point was to see what cosmic rays would do to living things, and this was part of what was called the Blossom project.

By NASA's and the Royal Observatory's accounts, it went about 67 miles up, and space is usually said to begin at about 62 miles up, so 67 miles puts those flies past the line, right? And then the canister came back down by parachute, and the flies were recovered alive.

Now, I'm saying about 67 miles on purpose. Sources don't all agree on the exact altitude, and you'll see a few different numbers out there, but most of them land around 67 or 68 miles, so about 67 is the safe way to say it. And I'm saying the first animals we know reached space on purpose too, because that's what the record shows.

## 04 The Monkeys

And the monkeys came after. On June 14th, 1949, a monkey called Albert II went up and reached about 83 miles, so he made it to space too, but he died on impact. So the flies came back alive in 1947, and two years later Albert II made it to space and didn't make it home.

## 05 Fair Credit to Laika

Now, to be fair to Laika, the first still stands, Laika was the first animal to orbit the Earth, in 1957, and that's a different and bigger thing than going straight up and coming back down. So the fair way to say it is that the fruit flies were the first animals we know reached space, and Laika was the first animal to orbit. Both of those are true, they're just not the same thing.

## 06 Who Was First

So who were the first animals in space? Fruit flies, on February 20th, 1947, riding a captured German V-2 from White Sands, New Mexico. By NASA's and the Royal Observatory's accounts they went about 67 miles up, past the line where space is usually said to begin, and they came back down on a parachute, alive. Albert II reached about 83 miles in 1949, and Laika became the first animal to orbit in 1957. Somebody did it first, and in this case it was a canister full of fruit flies.

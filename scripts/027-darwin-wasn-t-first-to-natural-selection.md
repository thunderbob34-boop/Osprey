# Darwin Wasn't First to Natural Selection

READING NOTES
- Runtime is about 5 minutes at a relaxed pace, roughly 827 spoken words. The research is narrow, so this one runs short on purpose rather than padding.
- Tone is calm and fair. This is a "first in print" story, not a theft story. Darwin comes out of it well, and so does Matthew.
- Say Arboriculture as AR-bor-ih-kul-cher, Ternate as ter-NAH-tay, Linnean as lin-NEE-an.
- Every hedge in here is on purpose. Read "first in print", "brief", "influenced almost no one", "independently", "a principle of natural selection" and "on the fringe" exactly as written. Never say Darwin stole, copied or plagiarized anything.
- Good on-screen text moments are 1831 On Naval Timber and Arboriculture, 28 years, April 7 1860 Gardeners' Chronicle, "anticipated", 1842 to 1844, June 18 1858, July 1 1858, and 1859 On the Origin of Species.

## 01 Cold Open

Twenty-eight years before Darwin's big book, a Scottish landowner put natural selection in print in a book about timber for navy ships, and Darwin admitted it. Charles Darwin is the name everybody connects with natural selection, and he earned that, and we're going to give him full credit for it. But he wasn't the first person to put the idea in print, and the man who was is a name almost nobody has heard, and Darwin himself said so in public.

## 02 The Famous Version

Here's the version most of us learned, in 1859 Charles Darwin published On the Origin of Species, and that book laid out natural selection, the idea that living things that are better suited to where they live tend to survive and pass that on, and over a long time that changes a species. And it's one of the most famous ideas in all of science, and it's Darwin's name on it.

And that's the version that stuck, because Origin of Species is a whole book built around that one idea. But a book in 1859 isn't where the idea first shows up in print, right? To find that you have to go back to 1831, and to a book about trees.

## 03 Patrick Matthew

His name was Patrick Matthew, and he was a Scottish landowner, and in 1831 he published a book called On Naval Timber and Arboriculture. Arboriculture just means growing trees, so this is a book about growing timber for navy ships, which is about the last place you'd go looking for one of the biggest ideas in biology.

But in an appendix at the back of that book, Matthew described a principle of natural selection. That's twenty-eight years before Origin of Species, and this isn't somebody's later claim about what he meant, the 1831 text exists, and you can read it, Darwin Online has it.

Now, to be fair to everybody, Matthew's passage was brief, it was a short stretch in an appendix, it was buried in a forestry book, and it influenced almost no one. So he was first in print, but it was brief, and it barely influenced anybody.

## 04 Darwin Admits It

And here's the part that matters, Darwin didn't hide from this, on April 7th, 1860, after Origin of Species was out, Darwin wrote a letter to the Gardeners' Chronicle, and in it he admitted that Matthew had anticipated him. Anticipated, meaning Matthew had got there first, and Darwin said he hadn't known about Matthew's book.

So this one doesn't need any guessing, Darwin acknowledged Matthew's 1831 priority in print in 1860. Britannica covers it, and the Darwin Correspondence Project at Cambridge has a letter Matthew sent to Darwin himself in 1864.

## 05 Not Plagiarism

Now, you'll see people online take this a step further and say Darwin stole it, and we're not going to say that. That plagiarism claim sits on the fringe, and it got a critical review in the journal Evolution.

And there's a good reason it doesn't hold up. Darwin had his own private sketches of the idea from 1842 to 1844, years before Origin of Species, so he was working it out on his own, he developed it independently. So the fair way to say it is that Matthew was first in print, Darwin got there on his own, and when Darwin found out about Matthew, he said so publicly.

## 06 And Then There's Wallace

And there's more, because Matthew isn't the only other name in this story, and the second one is Alfred Russel Wallace. Wallace reached the theory of natural selection independently too. Wallace sent an essay from Ternate, and it reached Darwin on June 18th, 1858.

So think about where Darwin was at that point, he'd had his sketches since the 1840s, he hadn't published it yet, and here comes somebody else with the same idea. And that's what pushed Darwin into it, on July 1st, 1858, a joint Darwin and Wallace paper was read at the Linnean Society. So Darwin's version first went public as a joint paper, with two names on it, the year before Origin of Species came out.

So that's two people besides Darwin. Matthew had it in print in 1831 and it went almost nowhere, and Wallace found it on his own in the 1850s and ended up sharing the stage with Darwin.

## 07 Who Did It First

Now, Darwin still gets the credit for a reason. He's the one who wrote the whole book on it, Origin of Species, and he worked out the idea on his own. But here's the plain version of who did it first, Patrick Matthew, a Scottish landowner, described a principle of natural selection in the appendix of a book about naval timber in 1831. Darwin worked out his own version privately in the 1840s, Wallace found it independently, and the two of them presented it together in 1858. Darwin published Origin of Species in 1859, and in 1860, in the Gardeners' Chronicle, he admitted Matthew had anticipated him. Somebody did it first in print, and it was Patrick Matthew.

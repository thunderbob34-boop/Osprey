## 01 Cold Open

You've probably heard the story of how pizza Margherita got its name, a pizza maker in Naples makes a pizza for the queen, and the palace sends him a thank-you letter. Well, one historian took a hard look at that royal letter, and he found its seal in the wrong place, and that's only the first problem with it. So today we're going to talk about a letter he thinks is probably fake, and about the fact that the pizza itself was being described in print about thirty years before the queen ever supposedly tasted it, so the pizza was there first, decades before the man in the story.

## 02 The Legend

So here's the legend, the way it usually gets told. In 1889, a pizzaiolo in Naples, a pizza maker named Raffaele Esposito, makes a pizza for Queen Margherita with tomato, mozzarella and basil, and those are the colours of the Italian flag, red, white and green, and the pizza gets named after her, and Esposito gets a thank-you letter from the palace.

And that letter is the important part, because that's the proof, right? A story about a queen eating pizza is just a story, but a letter from the palace with a seal on it is a document, and the document is what people point to when they say this is how the Margherita was invented.

## 03 The Letter

Now here's where it falls apart. A historian named Zachary Nowak looked hard at that letter, and he published his findings in 2014, in a paper called "Folklore, Fakelore, History: Invented Tradition and the Origins of the Pizza Margherita," and he found three problems with it.

The first one is the seal, the seal is in the wrong place. The second one is the handwriting, the handwriting doesn't match the official who supposedly signed it. And the third one is the name it's addressed to, it's addressed using his wife's surname, Brandi, and not his own.

Now think about that third one for a second. If the palace is writing to thank Raffaele Esposito, you'd expect it to be addressed to Raffaele Esposito, right? But it's addressed using the name Brandi, and that's the name that matters for the next part.

## 04 Who Made It Up

So if the letter is fake, who made it, and when? Nowak thinks it was probably made up later, and likely by the Brandi family, who ran the pizzeria from the 1930s, and that they did it as marketing. And a thank-you letter from the palace is about the best marketing a pizzeria could ask for, so that part isn't hard to understand.

Now, to be fair, I want to be really careful here. That's Nowak's argument, and it's a strong argument, but it's his argument, not proven. Nobody has proven who did it, and nobody has proven when. What Nowak has is a letter with the seal in the wrong place, handwriting that doesn't match, and the wrong surname on it, and from that he thinks it was probably made up later, likely by the Brandi family. So that's how I'm going to say it, probably fake, and likely the Brandi family, and not one word stronger than that.

## 05 The Pizza Was Already There

And there's more, because even if you set the letter aside, the pizza itself was already around. In 1858, in a book about the customs of Naples called Usi e costumi di Napoli e contorni, edited by Francesco de Bourcard, a Neapolitan writer named Emmanuele Rocco described the pizzas of Naples, and he described pizzas topped with basil, and thin slices of mozzarella, and sometimes tomato.

So that's 1858, which is three decades before the queen's supposed visit in 1889. Now, I want to be fair to the evidence here too. Rocco lists tomato as one topping you might get, not a required one, it's one of the combinations, and he doesn't call it a Margherita. He doesn't use that name. You'll find people online saying the 1858 book names the Margherita, and it doesn't, so don't repeat that. But basil and thin slices of mozzarella, and sometimes tomato, on a pizza in Naples, in print, in 1858, that's basically the pizza in the story, decades before the man in the story supposedly invented it for a queen.

## 06 Fair Credit

Now, to be fair to Raffaele Esposito, here's what we actually don't know. We don't know whether Esposito served the queen anything at all. That's genuinely unknown, so I'm not going to tell you he didn't, maybe he did. And Nowak's case is about the letter and how the story got told, it's not a case against Esposito himself. So the fair version is this, maybe Esposito made a pizza for a queen and maybe he didn't, nobody knows, but the letter that's supposed to prove it looks like it was probably made up later, and the pizza itself was already being described in Naples in 1858.

## 07 Who Was First

So here's where it lands. The famous story says Raffaele Esposito invented pizza Margherita for Queen Margherita in 1889, and the proof is a thank-you letter from the palace. But historian Zachary Nowak found that letter has the seal in the wrong place, handwriting that doesn't match the official who supposedly signed it, and an address using his wife's surname, Brandi, and he thinks it was probably made up later, likely by the Brandi family as marketing.

And the pizza itself, basil and thin slices of mozzarella and sometimes tomato, was described by Emmanuele Rocco in 1858, three decades earlier. Somebody did it first, and they were the pizza makers of Naples, decades before anybody wrote a letter about it.

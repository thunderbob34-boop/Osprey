# Atari Didn't Invent the Game Cartridge: Jerry Lawson's Channel F

## 01 Cold Open

Ask people where the video game cartridge came from and a lot of them will say the Atari 2600, which came out in 1977, but the Fairchild Channel F came out in November 1976, a year earlier, and it was the first home console with a microprocessor and game cartridges holding the software on a ROM chip. I want to be exact about that wording, because there's an older console people bring up, and we'll get to it, and the exact wording is the reason the claim holds up. So the Atari 2600 wasn't the first to do that, but it's easy to see why people remember it that way, and we'll give it its due too.

## 02 The Famous Version

The Atari 2600 is widely remembered as the console that brought the plug-in game cartridge into living rooms, and it came out in 1977. Now, to be fair to Atari, Smithsonian Magazine says the Atari 2600 went on to eclipse the Channel F, so it makes sense that the Atari is the one most people remember, right? It's the one that stuck, and the Channel F is one you've probably never touched.

## 03 The Channel F

The Channel F was released by Fairchild in November 1976, and that's the first home console with a microprocessor and interchangeable cartridges holding the game software in a ROM chip. The man who led it was Jerry Lawson, who lived from 1940 to 2011, and he led the Fairchild engineering team and is credited with developing the console. Now, I'm not going to call him the only inventor, because it was a team, and what the record says is that he led it.

And here's a detail I like, Fairchild's game cartridges were loaded in much the same way as an 8-track tape, and that comes from Smithsonian Magazine and from the archives at The Strong National Museum of Play, which holds Lawson's papers. So if you remember 8-track tapes, you already know roughly how you'd put a game into the Channel F.

## 04 The Odyssey Objection

Now here's the part that matters, because there's an objection you've probably already thought of, the Magnavox Odyssey. The Odyssey came out in 1972, and yes, it had plug-in cards, and that's why the claim has to be worded carefully. As the Smithsonian describes it, the Odyssey was built from discrete analog circuits, with no microprocessor, and its cards configured the console's circuits rather than holding game code. So the Odyssey had plug-in cards, and the Channel F was the first home console with a microprocessor and cartridges holding the game software on a ROM chip, and both of those things are true at the same time.

## 05 The Plain Version

And I'm not saying Atari copied Fairchild, because nothing in what we found says that, and we don't know whether Atari's designers drew on the Channel F at all. So here's the plain version, the Atari 2600 in 1977 is widely remembered as the console that brought the game cartridge home, and a year before it, in November 1976, Fairchild shipped the Channel F, the first home console with a microprocessor and game cartridges holding the software on a ROM chip, with Jerry Lawson leading the engineering team. Somebody did it first, and that's what this channel is about.

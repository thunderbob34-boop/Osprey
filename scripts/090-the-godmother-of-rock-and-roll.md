# The Godmother of Rock and Roll

## 01 Cold Open

Before Elvis shook his hips, a gospel singer was shredding an electric guitar in church, and in nightclubs. Elvis Presley gets called the King of Rock and Roll, and he earned his place, we'll give him his due. But years before the mid-1950s, a woman named Sister Rosetta Tharpe was already playing gospel on an electric guitar in clubs, and she's got a strong claim to being where a lot of this started.

## 02 The King

So here's the version most people know. In the mid-1950s Elvis makes his records for Sun and then RCA, and rock and roll explodes, and Elvis is the King of Rock and Roll. And it's easy to see why that story stuck, right? Elvis was huge, and when something gets that big, the person at the center of it ends up with the crown.

Now, to be fair to Elvis, he never claimed he invented rock and roll. The title King was given to him, he didn't give it to himself. So this isn't a story about Elvis taking credit, it's a story about who was playing this kind of music before he came along.

## 03 Sister Rosetta Tharpe

Sister Rosetta Tharpe was a gospel performer, and from the late 1930s she was playing electric-guitar gospel in clubs. So that's gospel music, on an electric guitar, in nightclubs, in the late 1930s, years and years before Elvis.

And in 1944 she recorded a song called "Strange Things Happening Every Day", with a pianist named Sammy Price, and some music scholars call that the first rock and roll song. Some, not all, and we'll come back to why that matters. But it's 1944, and that's about a decade before the mid-1950s explosion everybody remembers.

And she's known as the Godmother of Rock and Roll, that's the label that gets put on her, and it's not just fans saying it, Britannica uses it and NPR has called her that too, and PBS gave her a whole American Masters timeline of her career. So this isn't a fringe idea somebody cooked up, the big institutions are on her side on this one.

## 04 Who She Influenced

Now here's the part that matters. Sister Rosetta Tharpe influenced Elvis, she influenced Little Richard, and she influenced Johnny Cash. The influence on Elvis comes from her biographer, Gayle Wald, as reported by NPR, so according to her biographer, the King himself was influenced by her.

So you've got a gospel singer playing electric guitar in clubs in the late 1930s, recording a song in 1944 that some scholars call the first rock and roll song, and then influencing Elvis and Little Richard and Johnny Cash.

## 05 The First Record Problem

And there's more, because "the first rock and roll record" is one of those arguments that never really ends. There's another record that gets this title a lot, and it's called "Rocket 88", by Jackie Brenston and his Delta Cats, with Ike Turner. It was recorded on March 5th, 1951, at Sam Phillips's studio in Memphis, and it came out on Chess, and it's often billed as the first rock and roll record.

But even the Library of Congress, in its own essay on "Rocket 88", says rock and roll was a gradual evolution, and that there were earlier competitors. So there isn't one first record, right? It came together over time, from a lot of people. That's why we say some music scholars call Tharpe's 1944 song the first rock and roll song, and that she has a strong claim to it, instead of saying it's settled. But either way, "Strange Things Happening Every Day" was 1944 and "Rocket 88" was 1951, so if you're going to argue about firsts, she's in the argument, and she's early.

## 06 The Hall of Fame

Oh, one more thing. The Rock and Roll Hall of Fame inducted its first class in 1986, and Little Richard, one of the people she influenced, went in that year. Sister Rosetta Tharpe wasn't inducted until 2018. That's more than three decades after the first class, and when she did go in, she went in under a category called Early Influences, which, going by the name, is for the people who came before rock and roll and shaped it.

So the woman who influenced Elvis and Little Richard and Johnny Cash, the woman they call the Godmother of Rock and Roll, got into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame thirty-two years after Little Richard did.

## 07 Who Did It First

So let's put it in order. From the late 1930s, Sister Rosetta Tharpe was playing electric-guitar gospel in clubs. In 1944 she recorded "Strange Things Happening Every Day" with Sammy Price, and some music scholars call that the first rock and roll song. In 1951 "Rocket 88" was recorded in Memphis, and it's often billed as the first rock and roll record, though the Library of Congress calls it a gradual evolution. In the mid-1950s Elvis became the King, a title he was given, and he never claimed he invented rock and roll. In 1986 the Hall of Fame inducted its first class, and in 2018 Sister Rosetta Tharpe finally went in, under Early Influences.

Elvis is the King, and he earned his place, but she was playing this music years before him. Somebody did it first, and her name was Sister Rosetta Tharpe.

# Carrier's Air Conditioner Wasn't Built for People

## 01 Cold Open

Willis Carrier is the man people call the father of air conditioning, and here's the thing most people don't know, his air conditioner wasn't invented to cool people down, it was invented to keep paper from getting damp. And sixty years before Carrier, a doctor in Florida was already cooling rooms for his patients, and he got the first US patent for mechanical refrigeration, and he died broke. Now, to be fair, what Carrier did really matters, and we'll give him full credit for it, but he wasn't the first one to cool a room for people, and he didn't even name the thing.

## 02 Brooklyn, 1902

So here's the famous version, and it's a strange one, Willis Carrier's system for the Sackett-Wilhelms Lithographing and Publishing Company, a printing plant in Brooklyn, dates to July 17th, 1902, at least that's the date Carrier's own company history gives. And the problem they had wasn't that the workers were hot, the problem was the paper.

In humid summer air the paper soaked up moisture, and that threw off the inking. They were printing in layers, one colour on top of another, and if the paper soaks up water and changes even a little between layers, the colours don't line up. Now, you'll hear people say the paper was wrinkling, and that's not right, the problem was the paper taking on moisture and the colours not lining up.

So what Carrier did was blow air over chilled pipes to dry it out. Cold pipes pull moisture out of the air, the same way a cold glass gets wet on the outside on a summer day, and drier air meant the paper stayed the same between layers. And the air got cooler too, but the cooling was a side effect, right? The whole point was the humidity.

## 03 What Carrier Actually Did

Now, to be fair to Carrier, that's a really big deal. Controlling humidity is a huge part of what air conditioning actually does, it's not just about making air cold, it's about making air dry, and Carrier built a system to control that in 1902, and there's a patent for the air conditioner issued to Willis H. Carrier. So the father of air conditioning title isn't made up out of nothing, he earned a lot of it.

But he built it for paper, not people, and somebody else had already been cooling rooms for people sixty years earlier.

## 04 John Gorrie

In Apalachicola, Florida, there was a doctor named John Gorrie, and by about 1841 he was cooling the sickrooms of his patients, people with yellow fever and malaria. And the way he did it was ice, he'd hang a pan of ice from the ceiling, and air moved over it, and it cooled the room.

And Gorrie went further than that, he built a machine to make the ice himself. He filed his patent petition in 1848, and in 1851 he got US Patent Number 8,080 for it, and that's the first US patent for mechanical refrigeration, a machine that makes ice. The Smithsonian's National Museum of American History has his patent model, so this isn't a legend either, it's a patent with a model in a museum.

Now, I want to be careful here, because people sometimes say Gorrie invented the air conditioner, and that's not right either. His machine made the ice, and the ice cooled the rooms, so his machine wasn't an air conditioner. But a doctor deliberately cooling a room to help treat sick people, by about 1841, that's cooling a room for people, sixty years before Carrier cooled a room for paper.

## 05 How It Went for Gorrie

And this is the part that stays with you. Gorrie's chief backer, the man putting money behind the machine, died in 1851, the same year as the patent, and then the other investors fell away and the press mocked him. And Gorrie believed that the ice magnate Frederic Tudor, a man who made his money selling ice, was behind some of the smearing, and I want to be clear, that's what Gorrie believed, it's his suspicion, not something anybody's proven.

John Gorrie died broke in 1855, at fifty-one. He had the first US patent for mechanical refrigeration, he was cooling sickrooms for his patients by about 1841, and he died broke.

Now, he's not completely forgotten. There's a statue of John Gorrie in Statuary Hall, in the US Capitol, so his name is there, it's just not the name people say when they talk about air conditioning.

## 06 Who Named It

Oh, one more thing, even the phrase air conditioning isn't Carrier's. A textile engineer from Charlotte, North Carolina, named Stuart Cramer, coined the term air conditioning in 1906, and he used it in a patent filing and in a paper he gave to the American Cotton Manufacturers Association.

So the man called the father of air conditioning wasn't the first to cool a room for people, and he didn't even come up with the name, right? A doctor in Florida was cooling sickrooms sixty years before him, and a textile engineer in North Carolina named the whole thing.

## 07 Who Was First

So here's where it lands. Willis Carrier's 1902 system at the Sackett-Wilhelms printing plant in Brooklyn was built to keep paper from soaking up moisture, and the cooling was a side effect. But by about 1841, Dr. John Gorrie in Apalachicola, Florida, was cooling his patients' sickrooms with ice, and in 1851 he got the first US patent for mechanical refrigeration for a machine that made that ice, and he died broke in 1855. And in 1906, Stuart Cramer gave it the name air conditioning. Somebody did it first, and when it comes to cooling a room for people, a doctor in Florida named John Gorrie was doing it sixty years before Carrier.

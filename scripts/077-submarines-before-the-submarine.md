## 01 Cold Open

In 1776, one man in a one-man submarine slipped under the flagship of the British fleet in New York harbor, and that was the first submarine attack in history. It happened during the American Revolution, and it didn't work, the screw that was supposed to fix the bomb to the ship's hull wouldn't bite, so the bomb drifted off and went off harmlessly. The man who usually gets called the father of the modern submarine is John Philip Holland, and he earned that, but there were submarines in the water long before his.

## 02 The Famous Version

So the version most people know, if they know one, is John Philip Holland. Britannica calls him the father of the modern submarine and points to his 1898 boat, the Holland, and the USS Holland was commissioned by the US Navy on October 12th, 1900, as its first commissioned submarine.

And that's real, right? Holland is the one who made the practical, modern submarine, the kind a navy actually commissions, and nothing in this video takes that away from him. But a practical, modern submarine and the first boat to go underwater are two different things, and the second one goes back a lot further.

## 03 Drebbel on the Thames

The first name worth knowing is Cornelis Drebbel, a Dutch inventor who was working for King James I in England. Around 1620 he built what's been called a diving boat, sealed with greased leather, and moved it underwater along the River Thames using oars.

Now, to be fair, we don't know a lot about how it was built, because none of Drebbel's plans survive. The details you'll hear, like how deep it went, reportedly twelve to fifteen feet, and the route, reportedly from Westminster to Greenwich, and that King James himself watched, all come from later accounts. So those get a reportedly. And some people call Drebbel's boat the first practical submarine, and others call it the first navigable one, so the honest way to say it is that it's one of the earliest submarines we know about, around 1620, and leave it there.

## 04 The Turtle

And then there's the one that actually went to war. David Bushnell was a student at Yale, and between 1771 and 1775 he designed a one-man submarine called the Turtle. The idea was to get under an enemy ship and fix a bomb to its hull.

So on the night of September 6th going into the 7th, 1776, during the Revolution, a man named Ezra Lee climbed into the Turtle and went after the British flagship, HMS Eagle, in New York harbor. And he made it. He made it all the way under the flagship of the British fleet, in a one-man submarine, which is a pretty wild thing to do in 1776, right?

But the screw that was supposed to fix the bomb to the hull wouldn't bite. Now, exactly why it wouldn't bite is disputed, there are a couple of explanations floating around and the sources don't agree, so we're just going to say it wouldn't bite. And so the bomb drifted off and exploded harmlessly.

And here's the part that matters, it failed, but it still counts. The US Navy's own history command describes the Turtle as the first vessel used for an underwater attack, and it has published the naval documents from the time, and Smithsonian magazine calls the Turtle the world's first combat submarine, so that's why it's called the first submarine attack in history. That was 1776, and the US Navy didn't commission its first submarine until 1900, so that's 124 years.

## 05 Fair Credit to Holland

Now, to be fair to Holland, and this matters, being first underwater and being first to make it work as a modern weapon are not the same thing. Drebbel's boat moved under the Thames with oars, and the Turtle was a one-man boat that got under a ship and couldn't finish the job, and Holland built the kind of submarine the US Navy commissioned, and that's why he's the father of the modern submarine, and that title is his.

So the fair way to say it is that Drebbel and Bushnell built earlier submarines, not that they're the real inventors and Holland didn't earn it. He earned it. He earned it fair and square, he just wasn't the first one under the water.

## 06 Under the Water First

So the plain version goes like this. John Philip Holland is the father of the modern submarine, and the USS Holland was commissioned on October 12th, 1900, as the US Navy's first. But around 1620 Cornelis Drebbel had a boat sealed with greased leather moving underwater along the Thames, and in 1776 David Bushnell's Turtle, with Ezra Lee inside, made it under HMS Eagle in New York harbor in the first submarine attack in history.

Somebody did it first, and in this case it was a Dutch inventor on the Thames and a Yale student's one-man boat in New York harbor, long before Holland.

## 01 Cold Open

The Golden Gate Bridge's own operators now say something you don't hear very often, and it's that Joseph Strauss, the chief engineer whose name and statue are tied to the bridge, never officially recognized Charles Ellis, the engineer who directed the design calculations for the suspension bridge. The bridge district's own website says it this way, and I'll read it exactly, "the record clearly demonstrates that he deserves significant credit for the suspension bridge design." And that's careful wording, it says significant credit, it doesn't say sole designer, and we're going to stay as careful as the district is.

## 02 The Famous Version

Now, to be fair to Strauss, he was a real chief engineer and he was a real promoter, and he spent years getting the bridge funded and built. He also wasn't the only name that mattered, Leon Moisseiff was the consulting engineer, and Irving Morrow was the architect behind the Art Deco look. So nobody is saying Strauss did nothing, and nobody is saying one man designed the whole bridge, and that includes Ellis.

## 03 Charles Ellis

Charles Alton Ellis lived from 1876 to 1949, and Strauss hired him in the early 1920s. The district's website says 1921 and a Purdue-related source says 1922, so early 1920s is as exact as we can be. Ellis directed the design calculations for the suspension bridge, and he did that by applying Leon Moisseiff's deflection theory, which is about how a suspension bridge bends and moves under its load.

Then in December 1931 Strauss ended Ellis's involvement with the project. Why it ended isn't something we can say for sure, so we won't guess. Ellis later joined Purdue, where he was a civil engineering professor from 1934 to 1946.

## 04 What the Record Says

Here's what two separate institutions say. The Golden Gate Bridge Highway and Transportation District, the people who run the bridge, say Strauss never officially recognized Ellis, and that the record clearly demonstrates he deserves significant credit for the suspension bridge design. And Purdue University's archives, which have an entry on Ellis, also point to his design role, right? That's the bridge's operators on one side and a university archive on the other, and they line up on the main point.

What nobody has settled, at least in what we found, is exactly how the credit divides up between Ellis, Moisseiff, and Strauss, and that's why the district's wording matters, because it says significant credit and not all of it.

## 05 The Plain Version

So here's the plain version, Strauss was the chief engineer and the name tied to the Golden Gate Bridge, Ellis was the engineer he hired in the early 1920s who directed the design calculations, and the bridge district's own website says Strauss never officially recognized him. Ellis went on to teach at Purdue, and the district now says the record clearly demonstrates that he deserves significant credit for the suspension bridge design.

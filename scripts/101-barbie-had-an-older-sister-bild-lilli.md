# Barbie Had an Older Sister: Bild Lilli

READING NOTES
- Runtime is about 5 minutes at a relaxed pace, roughly WCOUNT spoken words.
- Tone is calm and matter-of-fact, and fair to Ruth Handler throughout. This is not a takedown, so keep the lawsuit section plain and flat.
- Say Bild as BILT, Lilli as LIL-ee, Zeitung as TSY-toong, Greiner as GRY-ner, Hausser as HOW-ser, Mattel as muh-TELL.
- Every hedge in here is on purpose. Read "modeled on", "inspired by", "reportedly", "according to Britannica", "the way the Smithsonian tells it", "sources differ" and "says" exactly as written. Do not swap in any stronger word, and do not say what Mattel paid, because the research has no figure. The first-year sales numbers are two different figures from two sources, so give both and do not pick one.
- Visuals: period photos of the dolls, newspaper cartoon images, paper doll images and text cards are all fine. Nothing sensitive here.
- Good on-screen text moments are 1945, 1955, 1956, March 9 1959, 1961, 1963, 1964, 11 inches, 300,000 and 351,000.

## 01 Cold Open

Ask a lot of people where Barbie came from and they'll tell you she was an original American invention, launched by Ruth Handler and Mattel in 1959, but Barbie was modeled on an older doll, one that started out as a gag gift for grown men and was already sold in West German stores in 1955, four years before Barbie came along. That doll was called Lilli, and she came from a newspaper cartoon. Now, Ruth Handler deserves real credit in this story and we'll get to that, but the doll that came first was Lilli.

## 02 The Famous Version

The famous version isn't wrong, it's just not the whole story. Ruth Handler co-founded Mattel in 1945 with her husband, and the way the Smithsonian tells it, she noticed her daughter Barbara playing with paper dolls of grown women and dreaming about what kind of woman she would become, and she decided there should be a doll that girls could put those hopes onto. The dolls on the market back then were mostly babies and toddlers, so a grown-up doll was something different, and she named the doll Barbie, after her daughter.

So when people give Handler and Mattel the credit for Barbie, they're giving it to the people who launched her, and that part is true. What the famous version leaves out is the doll she was modeled on.

## 03 Bild Lilli

Lilli started out as a flirtatious cartoon character in a German newspaper called Bild-Zeitung. Then in 1955 she became a doll, an eleven-inch plastic adult doll sold in West German stores, and Britannica describes that doll as a risque gag gift for men, so this was a joke for adults, right? The Strong National Museum of Play holds a 1955 Bild Lilli doll in its collection.

## 04 Handler Sees Lilli

Handler saw a Lilli doll on a 1956 trip to Europe, and the Library of Congress says that doll became the model for Barbie. Britannica's wording is that Barbie was modeled on Lilli, and I'm going to stick with that, modeled on, or inspired by, because that's what the sources say and I'm not going to go past it.

## 05 Toy Fair 1959

Barbie was first shown on March 9th, 1959, according to Britannica, at the American International Toy Fair in New York City, and the Smithsonian also puts her first display at the Toy Fair in March of 1959. Now, people in the toy business were skeptical, because this was a grown-up doll for girls in a market full of baby dolls, and Handler went ahead with it anyway.

It sold. Sources differ on the first-year number, the Smithsonian says three hundred thousand Barbie dolls were sold in the first year, and Britannica says three hundred fifty-one thousand at three dollars each, so I'll just say it was around three hundred thousand or a bit more.

## 06 The Lawsuit

Then there was a lawsuit. Reportedly, and this is according to Britannica, Lilli's makers, Greiner and Hausser, sued Mattel in 1961, the case was settled out of court in 1963, and in 1964 Mattel acquired the Lilli patents and copyright. I'm not going to give you a number for what Mattel paid, because I don't have one.

## 07 What Handler Did

Now, to be fair to Handler, being modeled on an older doll doesn't take away what she did. She pitched a glamour doll for American girls to colleagues who were skeptical about it, she's credited with the career-woman marketing that went with Barbie, and the doll she launched in 1959 was a hit. So this isn't a takedown of Handler, it's about a doll that was already on sale in West Germany before the American one showed up.

## 08 Close

So the plain version is, Lilli was a cartoon in a German newspaper, she was a doll in West German stores from 1955, Handler saw one on a trip to Europe in 1956, Barbie debuted in 1959, and in 1964 Mattel acquired the Lilli patents and copyright. Somebody did it first, and in this case the company that got the credit ended up with the rights to the original.

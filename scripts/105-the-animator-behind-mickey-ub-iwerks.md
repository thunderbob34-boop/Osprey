# The Animator Behind Mickey: Ub Iwerks

READING NOTES
- Runtime is about 2 minutes at a relaxed pace, roughly 317 spoken words.
- Tone is warm and fair to both men. This is shared credit, not a takedown, and both the Smithsonian and Britannica already name Iwerks.
- Say Iwerks as EYE-werks, and Ub as UB, rhyming with tub.
- Every hedge in here is on purpose. Read "according to the Smithsonian", "probably", "co-created", "tasked with designing" and "says" exactly as written. Do not say Walt had nothing to do with it, and do not say Steamboat Willie was the first anything.
- Visuals: period stills of the two men, early cartoon title cards and text cards. Nothing sensitive here.
- Good on-screen text moments are May 15 1928, November 18 1928 and 1947.

## 01 Cold Open

Walt Disney gave Mickey Mouse his voice, he voiced him until 1947, and a lot of people picture Walt as the man who created Mickey, but according to the Smithsonian, the man who was tasked with designing Mickey was Ub Iwerks, and Britannica says most of the early animation was probably Iwerks's work. Now, this isn't a case of somebody being erased, both of those sources name Iwerks, and Walt gets real credit too, but Iwerks is a name a lot of people haven't heard.

## 02 The Famous Version

The famous version is that Walt created Mickey, and there's real truth in that. Britannica says Mickey was co-created by Disney and Iwerks, so Walt is right there in the credit, and the way the sources describe it, he supplied the idea, the gags, the characterization and the voice. Mickey and Minnie first appeared in a cartoon called Plane Crazy, which Britannica dates to May 15th, 1928, and Steamboat Willie premiered on November 18th, 1928.

## 03 What Iwerks Did

According to the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History, Ub Iwerks was tasked with designing the character, with input from Walt. Britannica says Iwerks drew Mickey in the early years, and that most of the early animation was probably his work, and I'm keeping that word probably, because probably is Britannica's word.

## 04 Fair Credit

Now, to be fair to Walt, Britannica also says Iwerks worked under Walt's close supervision, so I'm not going to say Walt had nothing to do with it, because he had a lot to do with it, right? Co-created is the right word, and it's the word Britannica uses.

## 05 Close

So Walt supplied the idea, the gags, the characterization and the voice, and Iwerks, according to the Smithsonian, was the one tasked with designing Mickey, and Britannica says he probably did most of the early animation. Mickey was co-created by the two of them, and Iwerks is the half a lot of people haven't heard of.

## 01 Cold Open

Ask a lot of people who was first across the Atlantic by air and they'll say Charles Lindbergh, in 1927, but about eight years before that, in June of 1919, two men named John Alcock and Arthur Whitten Brown flew across it nonstop, and each of them carried a toy cat mascot. Now, to be fair to Lindbergh, his record is a different one, and it's a real one, and we'll get to it. But the first nonstop crossing came in 1919, and so did the first crossing by air of any kind, with stops.

## 02 What Lindbergh Did

Lindbergh made his flight on May 20th and 21st, 1927, according to the Smithsonian's National Air and Space Museum, flying solo, nonstop, from Long Island to Paris, about thirty-six hundred and ten miles, in about thirty-three and a half hours. That makes him the first SOLO nonstop crossing of the Atlantic, and that's his actual record, first solo nonstop. He also won the twenty-five thousand dollar Orteig Prize, which was for flying from New York to Paris, and the Smithsonian says that prize was offered back in 1919.

## 03 The Navy's NC-4

The first aircraft to fly across the Atlantic at all, with stops, was the US Navy's NC-4 flying boat. It left New York on May 8th, 1919, and went across by stages, by way of Newfoundland, the Azores, and Lisbon, and on to England, and one more thing about that trip, only one of the Navy's NC flying boats finished the whole thing.

## 04 Alcock and Brown

Then on June 14th and 15th, 1919, John Alcock and Arthur Whitten Brown flew from St. John's, Newfoundland, to a bog near Clifden, Ireland, in a Vickers Vimy biplane, and that was the first nonstop crossing. Sources differ a little on the flight time, so I'll say about sixteen hours, right? They won the Daily Mail's ten thousand pound prize, and they were knighted, and Britannica notes that Alcock died a few months later.

## 05 Two Toy Cats

Each of them carried a toy cat mascot, and I do mean toys, not live animals. Alcock's was named Lucky Jim, and the Science Museum Group has a Lucky Jim toy cat mascot in its collection, listed as Alcock's mascot on the 1919 flight. Brown's was named Twinkletoes, and the RAF Museum has Twinkletoes in its collection, so both cats survive in museums today.

## 06 Close

So Lindbergh's record is the first solo nonstop crossing, in May of 1927. The first aircraft across the Atlantic at all was the Navy's NC-4 in May of 1919, with stops along the way, and the first nonstop crossing was Alcock and Brown in June of 1919, and somebody did it first, and both of their cats are still around.

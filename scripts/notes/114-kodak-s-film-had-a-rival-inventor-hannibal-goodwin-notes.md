## Deepening (2026-10-02)
- New facts added: (1) The first Kodak (1888) used paper-based film, with price and mail-back process, and the transparent celluloid film came in 1889 (Smithsonian NMAH; Britannica; Eastman Museum listing). This also forced a small fix: the cold open now says "two years before Kodak sold any celluloid film". (2) House of Prayer rectory, Newark (NJ historical markers list; NJ State Library). (3) Patent assigned to Goodwin Film and Camera Company in 1898, and Ansco bought that company's stock in 1910 (court opinion only, flagged as single primary source). (4) Decree date September 9, 1913 and claim numbers 1, 6, 8, 10 and 12 (court opinion; the entry already listed the claim numbers). (5) Court's "more than ordinary merit" wording (court opinion only, flagged).
- Looked for but not usable: one extract said Goodwin's patent was not issued until 1889 because of application errors, which contradicts the patent record (1898), so it was discarded; Britannica extract saying Reichenbach patented a process "identical" to Goodwin's (single source, strong wording, kept off air); "Rev. Hannibal Goodwin invented celluloid film in 1887" (marker-list phrasing, not used because it would overstate); Goodwin's birth and death dates (not confirmed in any result); settlement amount and recipient (still conflicting; Ansco is now confirmed as the owner of the Goodwin company from 1910, but who was paid is still not stated); Goodwin's Sunday school and lantern-slide motive (not found); Eastman Museum Goodwin page (contents still not seen).

# Notes: The Pizza Margherita Letter Was Probably Fake

| Claim in script | Source(s) named in the research entry |
|---|---|
| The legend: 1889, Naples pizzaiolo Raffaele Esposito makes a tomato/mozzarella/basil pizza in the flag's colours for Queen Margherita and gets a palace thank-you letter | Entry's corrected on-air version (told as legend) |
| Zachary Nowak, 2014, "Folklore, Fakelore, History: Invented Tradition and the Origins of the Pizza Margherita" | Nowak 2014 (ResearchGate) |
| Seal in the wrong place; handwriting doesn't match the official who supposedly signed; addressed using his wife's surname, Brandi | Nowak 2014; Scott's Pizza Tours (secondary, summarises Nowak) |
| Nowak thinks it was probably made up later, likely by the Brandi family, who ran the pizzeria from the 1930s, as marketing; hypothesis, not proven | Nowak 2014; entry marks it "Inferred" |
| 1858, Emmanuele Rocco, in Usi e costumi di Napoli e contorni (vol. 2) edited by Francesco de Bourcard, described pizzas with basil, thin slices of mozzarella, sometimes tomato | storienapoli.it; angeloforgione.com (Italian secondary) |
| Tomato is optional in Rocco; he does not name it "Margherita"; the claim that he does is wrong | Entry's corrections list |
| Three decades before the queen's supposed visit | Entry's on-air version |
| Unknown whether Esposito served the queen anything at all | Entry's "Unknown" line |

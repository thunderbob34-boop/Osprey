# Notes: The Eiffel Tower Started as Two Employees' Sketch
Research entry: 100-episode-lineup.md, section "## History Channel-inspired additions (101–114)", entry "### 110."
Spoken words: 585

| Claim in script | Source(s) named in the research entry |
|---|---|
| Tower carries Eiffel's name, most people assume he designed it | Entry "Credited" line |
| Koechlin and Nouguier, two chief engineers at Eiffel's company | Eiffel Tower official site (history page; Koechlin page) |
| Koechlin made a rough calculation and sketch dated June 6, 1884 of an iron pylon about 300 meters tall | Eiffel Tower official site, "How the Eiffel Tower was invented" |
| Eiffel reportedly not interested but let his engineers keep working | Eiffel Tower official site (no primary document seen) |
| Sauvestre, in-house architect, reshaped it with arches, glass pavilions, three levels | Eiffel Tower official site |
| Patent filed September 18, 1884 | Eiffel Tower official site |
| 1889 Exhibition competition, 107 proposals; design submitted by Eiffel with Koechlin, Nouguier and Sauvestre | Eiffel Tower official site history page; Britannica |
| Eiffel's firm had bridge-building know-how, took the financial risk, won and built it | Entry "Hedges" (credit Eiffel) |
| Main sources do not say Eiffel stole or bought the idea | Entry "Hedges" |
| Koechlin trained at the Polytechnic in Zurich, learned graphic statics, and the sketch was built on that method | Eiffel Tower official site, Koechlin page; ETH Library "ETHeritage" blog on Koechlin's sketch (deepening) |
| Eiffel reportedly changed his mind once he saw Sauvestre's redesign, and a patent followed | Eiffel Tower official site, "How the Eiffel Tower was invented" (deepening; same single account as the "not interested" line, so hedged "reportedly") |
| Bartholdi first hired Viollet-le-Duc for the Statue of Liberty's internal structure; after his death Sept 17, 1879 turned to Eiffel, who designed a central pylon about 92 ft (28 m) and flexible girder skeleton | NPS Statue of Liberty pages (Viollet-le-Duc; Eiffel); Britannica Bartholdi / Statue of Liberty (deepening) |

Hedges kept: "reportedly" (both the lukewarm reaction and the change of mind), "the idea began with two of his engineers", "the design that won was submitted by the whole team", no patent holder named.
Left out on purpose (unverified / keep off air in research): who held the patent (sources disagree); the claim that Eiffel later bought the engineers' rights (non-strong source, not accepted); any "stole" or "bought" framing; the ResearchGate caption naming Nouguier as co-sketcher (supporting only).

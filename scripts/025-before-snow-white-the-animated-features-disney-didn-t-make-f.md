# Before Snow White: The Animated Features Disney Didn't Make First

READING NOTES
- Runtime is about 6 minutes at a relaxed pace, roughly 858 spoken words. The research is narrow, so this one runs short on purpose rather than padding.
- Tone is warm and matter-of-fact. Disney gets his real firsts said out loud, and Lotte Reiniger is the center of the story.
- Say Lotte Reiniger as LOT-tuh RYE-nig-er, Achmed as AHK-med, Quirino Cristiani as kee-REE-no kris-tee-AH-nee, El Apóstol as el ah-POSE-tole, Émile Cohl as ay-MEEL COAL, Fantasmagorie as fan-taz-ma-go-REE.

## 01 Cold Open

Snow White wasn't the first animated feature, and it may not even have been the second. Ask almost anybody what the first full-length animated movie was and they'll say Disney's Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs from 1937, and Disney did pull off some real firsts with that film, and we'll give him every one of them. But a full decade before Snow White, a woman in Germany finished an animated feature made entirely out of hand-cut silhouettes, and it still survives, and before her there's a film from Argentina that's reportedly even older and is now lost.

## 02 The Famous Version

Here's the version most of us grew up with, in 1937 Walt Disney released Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs, and it gets called the first animated feature film, and that line gets repeated so often that people just take it as a fact.

And you can see why it stuck, because Snow White really was a first in a few big ways. It's the first cel-animated feature with sound and in Technicolor. Cel animation, if you've never heard the word, is where the characters are painted onto clear sheets so they can be laid over a background and photographed frame by frame, and doing a whole feature that way, with sound and in full color, was a huge thing. So that's a real first, and nobody's taking it away, but the first animated feature, period, is a different claim, and that one doesn't hold up.

## 03 Lotte Reiniger

In 1926 in Germany, a filmmaker named Lotte Reiniger finished a film called The Adventures of Prince Achmed, and it wasn't drawn, it was cut, every figure in it was a silhouette cut by hand out of card, and then moved and photographed a little at a time to make it move on screen.

It took about three years to make, and here's the part that matters, The Adventures of Prince Achmed is the oldest animated feature film that still survives. The Library of Congress says it came ten years before Snow White, and the British Film Institute has it down as 1926, hand-cut card silhouettes, three years of work.

So a full decade before Disney's first feature, there was already an animated feature finished, and it was made by a woman cutting figures out by hand. Three years of it, three years of cutting and moving and photographing, a little bit at a time.

## 04 Even Earlier, and Lost

And there's more, because Reiniger's film is the oldest one that survives, and that's a careful way of saying it, because there's one that's reportedly even older, and it's lost.

On November 9th, 1917, a film called El Apóstol premiered in Buenos Aires, in Argentina, made by an animator named Quirino Cristiani. It's reportedly the first animated feature, but nobody can sit down and watch it today, because the film is lost, it was destroyed in a studio fire in 1926, so everything we know about it comes secondhand, from old ads and people who wrote about it, not from the film itself.

So that's why we say reportedly. If El Apóstol was a full animated feature, then Snow White was at least third, and if it wasn't, Prince Achmed is still a full decade ahead of Snow White, so either way Snow White isn't first.

## 05 Before Features at All

Oh, one more thing, because animated film didn't start with features. In Paris in 1908, a filmmaker named Émile Cohl made a short film called Fantasmagorie, and it's one of the earliest fully drawn animated films. You'll see people call it the first cartoon, and we're not going to say that, but it's a key early cartoon, and it came almost thirty years before Snow White.

So by the time Disney got to 1937, animation had already been around for decades, right? There were short cartoons going back to at least 1908, there was a feature in Argentina in 1917 that's reportedly the first, and there was a silhouette feature in Germany in 1926 that you can still watch today.

## 06 Fair Credit to Disney

Now, to be fair to Disney, Snow White is a landmark, it's the first feature to be cel-animated with sound and in Technicolor, and that's a real landmark. So when people say Snow White was first, they're not completely wrong, they're just leaving out some words, because it was the first cel-animated, sound, Technicolor feature, but it wasn't the first animated feature.

And when you leave out those words, you leave out Lotte Reiniger and her three years of cutting silhouettes, and you leave out Quirino Cristiani and a lost film from Buenos Aires.

## 07 Who Did It First

So here's the plain version, Émile Cohl made one of the earliest fully drawn animated films in Paris in 1908. Quirino Cristiani's El Apóstol premiered in Buenos Aires in 1917, it's reportedly the first animated feature, and it's lost. Lotte Reiniger finished The Adventures of Prince Achmed in Germany in 1926, after about three years of hand-cut silhouettes, and it's the oldest animated feature that still survives. Disney's Snow White came in 1937, the first cel-animated feature with sound and in Technicolor. Somebody did it first, and the oldest one you can still watch was made by Lotte Reiniger.

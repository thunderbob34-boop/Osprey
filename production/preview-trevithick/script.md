## 01 Before Stephenson

On February 21st, 1804, at an ironworks in south Wales, a Cornish engineer named Richard Trevithick was about to test a new machine. It was a steam engine on wheels, sitting on an iron track. Behind it were five wagons, loaded with ten tons of iron. And about seventy men climbed on for the ride. The engine pulled the whole load nearly ten miles, down the valley to Abercynon. It was the first known railway journey by steam locomotive. But there was a problem. The engine was too heavy, and the cast-iron rails kept cracking under it. Twenty-five years later, the Stephensons' engine Rocket won the Rainhill Trials, and their name went into the history books. Trevithick died broke in 1833, and local workers paid for his funeral. Somebody did it first. His name was Richard Trevithick.

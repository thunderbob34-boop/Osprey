# Somebody Did It First: podcast plan (draft, 2026-10-09)

**Decided by Gus (2026-10-09):**
- No camera at first. A camera comes later, if there's a studio worth showing.
- Focus on the product first.

## Episode flow (about 25–35 minutes solo; 40 with a guest)

| # | Segment | Length | What happens | Scripted? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Cold open | 20–30 s | One concrete line from the middle of the story ("In 1804, about seventy men climbed onto wagons behind an engine…"). No intro first. | Yes |
| 1 | Show intro | 10 s | "This is Somebody Did It First. I'm Gus." Short music sting. | Yes |
| 2 | The story everyone knows | 3–5 min | Gus reads the famous version straight, the way people believe it. No winking. | Yes, from our verified scripts |
| 3 | The turn | 5 s | "But somebody did it first." Sting. | Yes |
| 4 | Who really did it | 8–12 min | The real first: who, when, where, what happened, told in time order with numbers. | Bullet sheet, said in Gus's words |
| 5 | Why history forgot them | 5–8 min | The discussion: money, publishing, politics, luck. Later this is the guest segment (Gus's dad, then guest stars). | Talking points only |
| 6 | What we know vs. the legend | 1–2 min | The hedges said once and plainly ("the sources disagree on…"). | Sheet |
| 7 | The verdict | 30 s | One plain line on who deserves the credit. | Yes |
| 8 | Next time | 20 s | Tease the next episode, then one ask: subscribe or follow. | Yes |

**Rules carried over from the scripts:**
- Say each point once.
- Trust the audience.
- No "right?" and no asides.
- Hedges said once.

## Each episode, who does what
1. **Claude: the prep packet, one page plus the read-aloud script.** It holds:
   - the cold open;
   - the famous-story script;
   - the "who really did it" bullet sheet with a source for every fact;
   - the hedges to keep;
   - the things to stay away from;
   - 5 discussion questions;
   - the verdict line;
   - the next-time tease.
2. **Gus records.** Audio only, one take per segment is fine.
3. **Claude does the post work:**
   - transcribes the recording and fact-checks it against the packet, flagging anything to cut or correct;
   - sets chapter markers;
   - writes show notes with the sources;
   - picks 2–3 Short cuts;
   - builds the YouTube visual version (below);
   - writes 2 title and thumbnail options.
4. **Gus approves and publishes.**

## Where it's published
- **Audio host: Spotify for Creators.**
  - It's free and gives one RSS feed. From that feed we submit to Apple Podcasts, Amazon Music/Audible, Pocket Casts and the others.
  - Check its current terms before signing up.
- **YouTube, on the Somebody Did It First channel.**
  - YouTube is now the most-used podcast platform in the US (Edison Research, via secondary reports).
  - Don't rely on YouTube's automatic RSS import. It makes a static-image video, and the guides note those don't get the engagement real video does.
  - Instead, upload a visual version: Gus's audio over real photos, maps and the time bar, changing as each fact comes up. We already have the engine for this, and it needs no AI image credits.
  - Use the same chapters as the audio.
- **Shorts, Reels and TikTok:** 2–3 vertical clips per episode, captioned, with the real photo on screen.

## How it gets advertised (free first; paid only once an episode holds listeners)
1. **Clips are the engine.** Post 2–3 Shorts, Reels or TikToks per episode, spread across the week. Each one is a complete little story ending on "…and that's not even the person who did it first."
2. **Searchable titles.** Use "Who Really Invented the Railroad?" rather than "Episode 1: Trevithick". Choose the episode order by search demand (Icahn method), not by our list order.
3. **Launch with 3 episodes on the same day,** so a new listener can binge. After that, publish weekly on the same day.
4. **A one-time mention on Lumber Counter Talk and Heavier Runners** (community post and end screen). The audiences differ, so one mention is enough.
5. **History communities on Facebook groups and Reddit.**
   - Share the clip with its sources, and only where each group's rules allow it.
   - The sources are the hook: "every claim is sourced in the notes."
6. **Guests bring audiences.** The dad episodes first, then guest stars, and appearing as a guest on other small history podcasts.
7. **Paid ads later.** Once one episode shows good listener retention, run a small promoted-video test on that episode only.

## Pilot
Record 2–3 episodes before announcing anything. Episode 1 is Trevithick, because the research, photos, maps and time bar are already done.

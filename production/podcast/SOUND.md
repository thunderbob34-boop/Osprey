# How the podcast should sound (proposal, 2026-10-09; waiting on Gus)

**In one line:** someone who knows the real story telling it to a friend, with the receipts in his pocket.

## References
| Show | What we take | What we leave |
|---|---|---|
| **Our Fake History** (Sebastian Major) | The closest match to our premise: take a story everyone believes and sort out what's true. A model for the solo episodes and for saying "the story goes" vs. what the evidence shows. | Its multi-part series length. |
| **The Rest Is History** (Tom Holland and Dominic Sandbrook) | A model for the episodes with Gus's dad: two people who know their stuff, teasing each other, assuming the listener is smart, never lecturing. | Their tangents. |
| **Revisionist History** (Malcolm Gladwell) | "The thing you think you know is wrong", told with pace, a clear turn and a payoff. Music used sparingly. A model for the read-aloud story parts. | Heavy sound design. |
| **The Infographics Show** (measured 2026-10-03) | The cadence of the read-aloud parts: exact numbers, the story turning on "but", no recaps. | Its narrator-voice feel. |

**Not:**
- Hardcore History's length.
- The crude comedy of Behind the Bastards or The Dollop. The Dollop's structure, where one host reads a story to the other, is close to the planned dad format, so borrow that structure only.

## The sound
- **Pace.** Read-aloud parts a little slower than conversation. Leave a beat of silence before "But somebody did it first."
- **Energy.** Curious and satisfied, not outraged. "Somebody did it first" is a reveal, not a gotcha.
- **Music.**
  - One short theme (5–10 s) at the open and close.
  - One sting for the turn.
  - A low music bed only under the cold open and the famous story.
  - Nothing under the discussion.
  - No whooshes or sound effects.
- **Recording.**
  - A mic close to the mouth, in a small soft room (a closet full of clothes works).
  - Loudness gets set in the edit to the usual podcast target, about -16 LUFS.
- **Voice rules.** Say each point once and trust the audience. In the read-aloud parts, no "right?" and no asides.

## Fun (added 2026-10-09, after Gus asked "does our podcast include fun?")
**It should be fun.** The no-asides rules were written for the AI-narrated scripts, which came out stiff and condescending. In a podcast with Gus and his dad, the banter is the point. The Rest Is History is the model: two people who know their stuff, laughing at the absurd bits.

**Where the fun comes from:**
- **Gus's own reactions** in the your-words and discussion parts: opinions, disbelief, calling things ridiculous.
- **The absurd true details,** which history is full of. About seventy men hitched a ride on the first steam railway journey. Watt, by Trevithick's account, said he deserved hanging.
- **Recurring bits:**
  - **Legend or Fact?** A quick-fire round of 3–4 true/false claims; the guest guesses. It doubles as a Short.
  - **Credit rating.** Was the famous name's credit Stolen, Shared or Earned? It's the host's opinion, and the guest argues.
  - **Weirdest thing I found.** One strange true detail to close out the episode.
- **Teasing each other** once Gus's dad is on.

**The one line:** jokes and opinions never change the facts. Opinions are said as opinions, and every factual claim inside a joke still has to be true. The edit pass checks this.

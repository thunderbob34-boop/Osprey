# The Infographics Show: what they do (researched 2026-10-03)

## Sources
- Channel listing and captions, pulled 2026-10-03 with yt-dlp:
  - "The Mistake That Exposed Osama Bin Laden": 16 min, 889K views.
  - "The True Story of Hitler's Jewish Soldiers": 32 min, 247K views.
  - The best performer in the last 40 uploads was "He started his new job at 7:30 AM. By 9:45 AM, he grounded America." (2.5M views). Its captions were rate-limited.
- Production and format articles: facelesschannels.net case study; becomeviral.com case study; animotionsstudio.com; Deadline (FilmRise rights deal, Jan 2026).

## Production (from articles)
- Founded in 2011 by Andrej Preston. Main narrator: Josh Risser.
- Scripts run 2,000–4,000 words in a conversational tone. The visuals carry the detail: maps, timelines, simple diagrams.
- Flat 2D animation with recurring faceless characters and icon sets, made in Illustrator and After Effects by a team of 5–15 animators.
- About 15M subscribers. Uploads are near-daily, but each video takes weeks to make.

## Narration, measured from the two transcripts

| Measure | Bin Laden (16 min) | Hitler's Jewish soldiers (32 min) |
|---|---|---|
| Pace | 177 wpm | 182 wpm |
| Mean sentence length | 11.6 words | 17.2 words |
| Sentences of 8 words or fewer | 40% | 16% |
| Uses of "you" | 3 | 1 |
| Questions | 3 | 8 |
| Numbers spoken | 42 | 51 |
| Recap phrases | about 0 | 2 |
| "But" | 23 | 56 |

What the numbers show:
- **Pace.** Both run at about 180 wpm, faster than our Holden narration (168).
- **Sentences.** Short and long are mixed.
- **Third person.** "You" almost never appears; the narration is a third-person story.
- **Questions** are rare.
- **Numbers** are everywhere: exact times, counts and ages.
- **Recap phrases** ("as mentioned", "remember", "in other words") almost never appear. Nothing is said twice.
- **Hedges** are light, attributed, and said once: "according to", "it is believed", "approximately".
- **The story turns on "but".**

Openings:
- Start inside a scene with one named person and an odd concrete detail. For example, Bin Laden in his compound while his kids play Counter-Strike.
- Or start with a one-line reversal: "The Nazis thought they had found the perfect Aryan soldier. They were wrong."

Middle:
- Moment-by-moment narration in strict time order, with exact times ("Approaching 1:00 a.m. local time").
- Short beats at the tense moments: "No one spoke."

Endings:
- One plain line of meaning, then a pointer to the next video ("Watch ... to find out what happened next").

## Shorts
Their recent Shorts are mostly "what happens if / when" body and science topics. History Shorts do well too: "The Brazen Bull" has 511K views.

## A Short, scene by scene ("The Brazen Bull", 511K views; Higgsfield video analysis)
- 23 seconds, 6 scenes, one about every 4 seconds.
- One sentence per scene, and the picture shows exactly that sentence.
- The camera holds still in every shot: wide establishing shot, then medium, close-up, close-up, medium close-up, extreme close-up.
- The first line names the thing and says why it matters ("a terrifying execution device used by the ancient Greeks").
- There is no outro in the clip analysed.

## What we take
- Tell one person's story, on one day, in time order. Start at the moment, not with a lecture.
- Say each fact once. Hedge once, at the source ("Nowak thinks..."), and move on.
- Let the screen carry the detail (maps, dates, the time bar). The narration doesn't describe what's on screen.
- Keep the pace brisk and turn the story on "but".
- End with one plain line, then point to the next video.

## Open
Whether to add simple character animation (their style) to act out the scenes, with real photos for the real people and machines. Gus liked their "basic animations that go with the story", but also rejected cartoon reconstructions of historical technology.

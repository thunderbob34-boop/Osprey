# Project brief for Hoosier Goose Co.: "Somebody Did It First"

Updated 2026-10-09 by the Claude Code session building it. Hand this to Cowork so the umbrella company knows the project exists, where it stands, and where everything lives.

## What it is
- **The brand.** Hoosier Goose Co.'s third media brand, after Lumber Counter Talk and Heavier Runners.
- **The premise.** Someone famous gets the credit for an invention or a first, but someone else did it first. Each episode tells the story everyone knows, then the real first and why history forgot them.
- **Credibility is the main asset.** Every claim is sourced and every hedge is kept. That is what separates the show from AI slop.
- **The YouTube channel is secured.** It sits on the same Google account as Lumber Counter Talk and Heavier Runners, as a Brand Account.

## Current direction: an audio-first podcast hosted by Gus (decided 2026-10-08/09)
- **Host and guests.** Gus hosts and voices it himself. Later guests: Gus's dad, who knows a lot of history, then guest stars.
- **No camera for now.** It may come later, if there's a studio worth filming.
- **Episode flow.** Cold open, then the famous story read aloud, then "But somebody did it first." Then who really did it, why history forgot them, what we know vs. the legend, and a plain verdict. It closes with a tease for next time. About 25–35 minutes.
- **Where it's published.**
  - **Audio:** Spotify for Creators, which is free and gives an RSS feed for Apple, Amazon and the others. Confirm its current terms before signing up.
  - **YouTube:** a "visual podcast" version, with Gus's audio over real photos, maps and a running time bar.
  - **Clips:** 2–3 vertical clips per episode for Shorts, Reels and TikTok.
- **Promotion.** Free first: the clips, searchable titles, a 3-episode launch then weekly, one mention on the sister channels, history groups (within their rules), and guests. Paid ads only after an episode proves listeners stay to the end.
- **Pilot.** Record 2–3 episodes before announcing anything. Episode 1 is Richard Trevithick (the steam locomotive, 25 years before Stephenson's Rocket). Its prep sheet is done.
- **Sound references:**
  - **Our Fake History** for the solo format;
  - **The Rest Is History** for the episodes with Gus's dad;
  - **Revisionist History** for the read-aloud story parts.

## Standing rules (from the channel memory file, which is the law for this brand)
- **Facts.** Every factual line traces to strong sources; Wikipedia never counts. Keep every hedge exactly ("about", "first known", "the story goes"), said once.
- **Fair credit.** The famous person gets their real due ("they built what lasted"), never "fraud", unless a fraud is documented.
- **Voice.**
  - Plain talk; say each point once; trust the audience.
  - No "right?", no narrator asides and no slogan lines in the read-aloud parts.
  - The rest of the show should be fun: reactions, opinions and recurring bits (Legend or Fact?, the Stolen/Shared/Earned credit rating). Jokes never change the facts.
  - Banned words: "genuinely", "dive in", "game changer", "unpack", "in today's video", "level up". No em dashes in spoken text.
- **On screen.** Never claim more than the narration: no added places or dates. No realistic AI "photo" of a real person or event. Every image credited.
- **Holds.** The pizza Margherita episode (and its Short) does not air until two sources are pulled: Zachary Nowak's paper and the 1858 Naples text. An 1880 newspaper item also has to be checked.
